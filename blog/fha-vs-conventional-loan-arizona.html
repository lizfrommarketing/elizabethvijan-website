FHA vs. Conventional Loan in Arizona: Which Is Better in 2026? Down Payments, Mortgage Insurance and Loan Limits

Is an FHA or conventional loan better? FHA often fits better when your credit score is below 620 or your savings are thin; conventional usually wins when your credit is strong, because its mortgage insurance can come off. FHA allows 3.5% down with a 580 credit score, while conventional loans can start at 3% down for eligible buyers through Fannie Mae's 97% loan-to-value options.

The Arizona twist is price. HUD's 2026 FHA limit for a one-unit home in Maricopa County is $557,750, while the conventional conforming limit is $832,750. With Scottsdale's October median single-family price at $1,225,000 and Chandler's at $535,000 (Phoenix REALTORS), where you shop can make the decision for you.

FHA vs. Conventional at a Glance

FHA

  • Minimum down: 3.5% with a 580+ score; 10% with 500-579
  • 2026 one-unit limit, Maricopa County: $557,750
  • Mortgage insurance: 1.75% upfront plus 0.55% a year with under 5% down (30-year)
  • Can it end? After 11 years with 10%+ down; otherwise it lasts the loan term

Conventional

  • Minimum down: 3% for eligible buyers
  • 2026 conforming limit: $832,750
  • Mortgage insurance: no upfront premium; PMI cost varies with credit and down payment
  • Can it end? Request at 80% of original value; automatic at 78%

How Mortgage Insurance Really Differs

FHA MIP

Per HUD Mortgagee Letter 2023-05, FHA charges 1.75% upfront and, on a 30-year loan with more than 95% loan-to-value, 0.55% a year. With less than 10% down, that annual premium stays for the mortgage term.

Example: on a $500,000 purchase with 3.5% down, the base loan is $482,500. Upfront MIP is about $8,444, and annual MIP starts around $2,654, or roughly $221 a month (math based on HUD's rates).

Conventional PMI

The CFPB explains you can ask to cancel PMI when your balance is scheduled to reach 80% of the home's original value, and it must end automatically at 78% if you're current. See how to remove PMI.

Down Payment and Credit Rules

  • FHA: HUD's handbook allows 96.5% loan-to-value on a purchase with a 580+ score; scores from 500 to 579 are capped at 90%
  • Conventional 3% down: Fannie Mae's HomeReady (income up to 80% of area median) or 97% LTV Standard, where at least one borrower must be a first-time homebuyer
  • Homebuyer education: required by Fannie Mae above 95% LTV when all occupying borrowers are first-time buyers
  • Credit: Fannie Mae's minimum is 620 on manually underwritten fixed-rate loans; Desktop Underwriter has no set minimum, though lender overlays are common. More in what credit score you need to buy a house in Arizona

What Sellers Notice

Sellers sometimes ask whether an FHA offer is riskier. The loan itself is sound, but the FHA appraisal has extra property observations:

  • The appraiser must observe attic interiors, per HUD's handbook
  • On homes built after 1978, defective exterior paint that exposes the surface must be repaired; older homes fall under lead-based paint rules

In a market where Scottsdale sellers received 96.8% of list price in October, a clean, well-documented FHA offer can compete well.

Which Is Better for You?

  • Choose FHA if your score is below 620, your debt ratios are tight, or you want the most flexible approval
  • Choose conventional if your score is strong, you're near 10-20% down, or your price is above $557,750
  • Above $832,750: you're in jumbo territory; read our jumbo loan limit guide
  • Compare Loan Estimates: with the 30-year fixed averaging 7.11% on Nov. 12 (Freddie Mac), compare total monthly cost including insurance, not just the rate

Frequently Asked Questions

Is an FHA or conventional loan better?

FHA helps with lower scores and smaller savings; conventional is often cheaper for strong credit because PMI can end.

What is the FHA loan limit in Maricopa County for 2026?

$557,750 for one unit, per HUD. The conforming limit is $832,750.

Can you remove mortgage insurance on an FHA loan?

After 11 years with at least 10% down; otherwise it lasts the loan term. Many owners refinance later.

What credit score do you need for FHA vs. conventional?

FHA: 580 for 3.5% down. Fannie Mae: 620 for manual underwriting; no set minimum in DU.
