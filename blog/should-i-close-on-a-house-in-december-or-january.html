Should I Close on a House in December or January? Taxes, Holiday Timing and Arizona Property Tax Proration

Should you close on a house in December or January? Close in December if you'll itemize deductions for 2026 and want this year's tax benefits; otherwise, choose the date that best fits your move, your rate lock and your cash. A December closing can let you deduct qualifying points, your share of property taxes, and the mortgage interest you pay this year on your 2026 return.

The catch: those deductions only help if your itemized total beats the 2026 standard deduction: $32,200 for married couples filing jointly and $16,100 for single filers, per the IRS. Many buyers won't clear that bar in a partial year, so for them, timing is more about logistics than taxes.

The Tax Case for December

Points. IRS Publication 936 lets you deduct the full amount of points in the year paid on a loan to buy your main home if you meet all of its tests and itemize. Close in December and that deduction lands on your 2026 return.

Property taxes. Per Publication 530, taxes are divided by days of ownership: the seller is treated as paying up to but not including the date of sale, and the buyer from the date of sale onward. Your share is deductible if you itemize.

Mortgage interest. Interest on up to $750,000 of acquisition debt ($375,000 if married filing separately) is deductible for loans taken out after Dec. 15, 2017. See our mortgage interest deduction guide.

State and local tax limit. For 2026, the IRS lists a $40,400 SALT limit ($20,200 married filing separately), reduced for modified AGI over $505,000, but not below $10,000.

When January Makes More Sense

  • You'll take the standard deduction anyway: December offers little tax advantage
  • Cash flow: a later closing gives you more time to gather funds and enjoy the holidays without moving boxes
  • Next year's itemizing: if you expect to itemize in 2027 but not 2026, January points and interest may do more for you
  • Inventory: with Scottsdale averaging 75 days on market in October 2026 (Phoenix REALTORS), January buyers still have room to negotiate

Arizona Property Tax Proration

Arizona bills property taxes in two halves: due Oct. 1 and March 1 (A.R.S. 42-18052). Taxes for 2026 aren't fully paid until the second half in spring 2027, so on a December closing:

  • The seller typically credits the buyer for the seller's share of 2026 taxes not yet paid
  • The buyer then pays the second half (directly or through escrow)
  • Paying the full year by Dec. 31 waives interest on a late first half, per the Treasurer

For dates and delinquency rules, read when are property taxes due in Maricopa County.

Holiday Logistics in Arizona

  • Target dates to avoid: Dec. 24, Dec. 25, Dec. 31 and Jan. 1; many offices close or run short-staffed
  • Rate locks: Freddie Mac's survey had the 30-year fixed at 7.11% on Nov. 12, 2026; make sure your lock covers holiday delays
  • Funding cutoffs: ask your lender and title company for their last funding and recording day of the year
  • Utilities: City of Scottsdale offices close Dec. 25 and Jan. 1, so schedule service starts in advance
  • Movers: book early; holiday weeks fill quickly

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it better to close on a house in December or January?

December helps if you itemize for 2026. If you take the standard deduction, choose the date that fits your move.

Are mortgage points deductible in the year you buy?

They can be, in full, if you meet the IRS Publication 936 tests and itemize.

How are property taxes split when you close in December in Arizona?

By days of ownership. A December buyer usually receives a seller credit for unpaid 2026 taxes.

Do title companies close between Christmas and New Year's?

Many do, but build in extra days and confirm funding cutoffs.

Buying or Selling Around the Holidays?

I'll coordinate with your lender, title company and CPA so your closing date works for your taxes and your calendar.
