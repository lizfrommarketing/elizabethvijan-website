How to Buy a Foreclosed Home in Arizona: Trustee Sales, Bank-Owned Homes and the Rules That Protect You

How do you buy a foreclosed home in Arizona? You can buy before the sale (usually a short sale), bid at the trustee's sale auction with a $10,000 deposit and next-day payment, or buy afterward once the lender owns it and lists it like any other home. For most buyers, the before-and-after routes are far safer than the auction.

The timeline is set by law: Arizona's trustee's sale can be held no sooner than the 91st day after the notice of sale is recorded, under A.R.S. 33-808, so there's a defined window to act.

Data note: Procedures are from the Arizona Revised Statutes, Title 33, Chapter 6.1 (sections 33-807, 33-808, 33-810, 33-811 and 33-813), read November 18, 2026. Rates are from Freddie Mac's PMMS (Nov. 12, 2026).

How Arizona Foreclosures Work

Arizona home loans are commonly secured by a deed of trust, which gives the trustee a power of sale after a default (A.R.S. 33-807). That lets the lender foreclose through a nonjudicial trustee's sale rather than a lawsuit.

Key notice rules under A.R.S. 33-808:

  • Recorded notice: filed with the county recorder where the property sits
  • Posting: on the property at least 20 days before the sale
  • Publication: in a newspaper once a week for four consecutive weeks
  • Earliest sale date: the 91st day after the notice is recorded
  • When and where: a business day between 9 a.m. and 5 p.m., at the property, a superior court building or the trustee's place of business

Route 1: Buy Before the Sale

Until the auction, the owner still owns the home and can sell it. These are often short sales, where the lender agrees to accept less than the payoff.

What to know:

  • Lender approval is required for a short sale, so timelines can stretch
  • The owner can reinstate: under A.R.S. 33-813, the owner or certain lienholders can cure the default until 5 p.m. on the last business day before the sale, which cancels it
  • You can inspect and finance just like a normal purchase

Route 2: Bid at the Trustee's Sale

This is the fastest and riskiest route. Under A.R.S. 33-810 and 33-811:

  • $10,000 deposit to bid: every bidder except the lender must provide it, in a form the trustee accepts
  • Highest bidder wins; only the lender can bid with a credit instead of cash
  • Bids are irrevocable until the sale is complete
  • Pay in full by 5 p.m. the next business day. Miss it, and the deposit is forfeited
  • Trustee's deed is submitted for recording within seven business days after payment

The title catch

The trustee's deed transfers the home without a right of redemption and clear of liens junior to the deed of trust, but still subject to liens senior to it (A.R.S. 33-811). Order a title search before you bid. You also typically can't tour the interior or secure a traditional mortgage on that timeline, so auction buyers usually bring cash.

Postponements happen

The trustee can postpone by public announcement, but the new date must be within 90 calendar days (A.R.S. 33-810). Check the status the day of the sale.

Route 3: Buy After the Sale (Bank-Owned)

If no one outbids the lender, it takes the home back and usually lists it through an agent. These bank-owned or REO homes are the most familiar path:

  • Financing is possible, at today's 7.11% average 30-year rate (Freddie Mac, Nov. 12, 2026)
  • Inspections are allowed, though sales are often as-is; see our home inspection cost guide
  • Title insurance gives you protection; see who pays title insurance in Arizona
  • Expect lender addenda that change standard contract terms, so read them closely

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you buy a foreclosed home in Arizona?

Buy before the sale, bid at the trustee's sale with a $10,000 deposit, or buy the bank-owned home afterward.

How long does the foreclosure process take in Arizona?

The sale can be held no sooner than the 91st day after the notice is recorded.

Can you buy a foreclosure before the auction?

Yes, usually as a short sale, though the owner can still reinstate until the day before.

Do you get a clear title at an Arizona trustee's sale?

Not always. Senior liens survive the sale.
