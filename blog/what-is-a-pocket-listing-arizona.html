What Is a Pocket Listing? Are Pocket Listings Legal in Arizona? Office Exclusives, Coming Soon and What Sellers Give Up

What is a pocket listing? It's a home sold privately, marketed to a limited circle of agents and buyers instead of through the MLS. In Arizona, the Arizona Regional Multiple Listing Service (ARMLS) allows two formal versions: an office exclusive, which isn't shared through the MLS or publicly marketed, and a Coming Soon listing, which is filed in the MLS but held from public websites for up to 30 days.

Are they legal in Arizona? Yes, within limits. Both require a signed seller disclosure. And under ARMLS's Clear Cooperation rule, once a home is marketed to the public, the listing must be submitted to the MLS within one business day.

The Three Ways to List in Arizona

1. Fully on the MLS (active)

The listing goes to every ARMLS member and syndicates to public sites through IDX and portals.

2. Coming Soon (delayed marketing)

  • Filed with ARMLS and visible to MLS agents
  • Held from IDX and public portals for up to 30 days
  • Moves to active automatically on day 31 if left in Coming Soon
  • A property can be Coming Soon for only 30 of every 45 calendar days
  • Once it leaves Coming Soon, it can't go back
  • The listing firm can still market it consistent with the seller's choice

3. Office exclusive

  • Not disseminated to other MLS members and not publicly marketed
  • No yard sign, public website, email blast or multi-brokerage sharing
  • One-to-one broker conversations don't count as public marketing, per NAR

What Counts as "Public Marketing"

ARMLS's Clear Cooperation rule lists:

  • Flyers in windows and yard signs
  • Public-facing websites, including brokerage sites, IDX and VOW
  • Email blasts
  • Multi-brokerage listing-sharing networks
  • Apps available to the public

Do any of these, and the listing must go to the MLS within one business day.

The Disclosure You'll Sign

For an office exclusive or Coming Soon listing, ARMLS requires a seller-signed disclosure covering:

  • Your professional relationship with the listing broker
  • That you understand the MLS benefits you're waiving or delaying, such as broad, immediate exposure
  • Your decision not to have the home publicly marketed now

Pros and Cons for Arizona Sellers

Why sellers choose it

  • Privacy: fewer public photos and showings
  • Security: useful for high-profile owners or occupied luxury homes
  • Timing: test price or finish projects before going live

What you give up

  • Exposure. Fewer buyers see it, which can mean fewer offers
  • Price discovery. Without broad competition, it's harder to know you got top dollar
  • Leverage. With fewer buyers competing, there's less to negotiate with

In Scottsdale, Phoenix REALTORS reports 1,480 single-family homes for sale in October 2026, 4.2 months of supply, and sellers receiving 96.8% of list price. In a market with that much competition, exposure matters. See how to sell fast in a slow market and is it a good time to sell.

Questions to Ask Before You Go Private

  1. Which option are we using: office exclusive or Coming Soon?
  2. What's the plan for day 31 if we haven't sold?
  3. How will we test price without full market exposure?
  4. Who can show it, and how will showing requests be handled?

Buying a Pocket Listing

  • Ask the listing agent whether it's an office exclusive or Coming Soon
  • Ask about representation: if the listing agent works both sides, read up on dual agency in Arizona
  • Pull your own comps. Off-market doesn't mean underpriced

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a pocket listing in real estate?

A home marketed privately instead of on the MLS; formally an office exclusive or Coming Soon listing.

Are pocket listings legal in Arizona?

Yes, within ARMLS rules, with a signed seller disclosure.

How long can a home be Coming Soon in ARMLS?

Up to 30 days, then it goes active automatically on day 31.

Should I sell my house as a pocket listing?

It can help with privacy or timing, but you give up broad exposure.
