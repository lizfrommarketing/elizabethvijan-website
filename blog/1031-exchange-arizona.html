1031 Exchange in Arizona: The 45- and 180-Day Rules, What Qualifies and How State Tax Works

How does a 1031 exchange work in Arizona? It follows federal law. You sell investment or business real property and buy like-kind real property, deferring the gain. In a deferred exchange, you must identify replacement property in writing within 45 days and close on it within 180 days or by your tax return's due date, whichever is earlier. A qualified intermediary holds the money in between.

Does Arizona add its own rules? Arizona's income tax starts from federal adjusted gross income (A.R.S. 43-1001), so a gain deferred federally generally stays out of that starting figure too.

Data note: Rules come from IRS Publication 544 (2025), the Instructions for Form 8824 (2025) and A.R.S. 43-1001. This is general information, not tax advice. No market statistics are included in this post.

1031 Exchange Rules at a Glance

  • Qualifies: real property held for business or investment
  • Doesn't qualify: your personal residence, or property held primarily for sale
  • Identify: within 45 days, in a signed writing
  • Close: within 180 days or by your return's due date (with extensions), whichever is earlier
  • Identification limit: 3 properties, or any number up to 200% of the value you gave up
  • Middleman: a qualified intermediary; your agent or a relative can't serve
  • Report: IRS Form 8824

What Qualifies as Like-Kind

Real Property Only

Since 2018, section 1031 applies only to real property held for use in a trade or business or for investment. Equipment and vehicles no longer qualify, and neither does real property held primarily for sale, such as a fix-and-flip.

Broad Within Real Estate

Most investment real estate is like-kind to other investment real estate, so a Scottsdale rental house can generally be exchanged for a Phoenix duplex or commercial property. But U.S. property and foreign property are not like-kind.

Homes and Vacation Rentals

A home used solely as your personal residence doesn't qualify. For a dwelling unit you've rented out or used personally, the IRS points to Revenue Procedure 2008-16 for a safe harbor. Read our second home guide if you're weighing a part-time residence.

The Deadlines, Step by Step

  1. Sell the relinquished property, with proceeds going to the qualified intermediary
  2. Day 45: identify replacement property in writing, signed, described by address or legal description
  3. Day 180 (or your return's due date, if earlier): receive the replacement property
  4. File Form 8824 with that year's return, and for two years after if a related party is involved

Miss a deadline and the transaction is treated as a sale.

The Identification Rules

  • Three-property rule: up to 3 properties, regardless of value
  • 200% rule: any number, if their total value is no more than double what you gave up
  • 95% exception: if you go over, it can still work if you acquire at least 95% of the total value identified

Where Exchanges Go Wrong

  • Touching the money. Actual or constructive receipt of the proceeds ruins the deferral, which is why a qualified intermediary is standard. Related parties and your agents are "disqualified persons" and can't be your QI.
  • Taking boot. Cash or other non-like-kind property you receive is taxable, and part may be depreciation recapture.
  • Mixing up closing costs. Brokerage commissions, attorney fees and deed preparation fees count as exchange expenses. Property tax and rent prorations, security deposits and repairs don't.
  • Related-party flips. If you exchange with a relative and either of you sells within 2 years, the deferred gain generally comes due.

What About Arizona Tax?

Under A.R.S. 43-1001, Arizona gross income for individuals is federal adjusted gross income, subject to Arizona's adjustments. In practice, a properly completed federal exchange generally keeps the deferred gain out of Arizona's starting point. Taxable boot doesn't get that treatment. See our Arizona capital gains tax guide for how a regular sale is taxed.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the 1031 exchange rules in Arizona?

Federal rules apply: investment real property, 45 days to identify, 180 days to close.

Can I do a 1031 exchange on my primary residence?

No. A home used solely as your residence doesn't qualify.

How many properties can I identify in a 1031 exchange?

Three, or any number up to 200% of the value you gave up.

Does Arizona tax 1031 exchange gains?

Arizona starts from federal AGI, so deferred gain generally isn't included.
