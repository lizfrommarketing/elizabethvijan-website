How Much House Can I Afford in Arizona? 2026 Math at Today's Rates for Phoenix and Scottsdale Buyers

How much house can you afford in Arizona? Start with two numbers: your gross monthly income and your lender's debt-to-income (DTI) limit. Fannie Mae caps total DTI at 36% for manually underwritten loans, up to 45% with strong credit and reserves, and up to 50% through its automated underwriting. At Freddie Mac's 7.18% average 30-year rate (Nov. 5, 2026), every $100,000 borrowed costs about $677 a month in principal and interest.

The shortcut: a $100,000 salary at 36% DTI supports about $3,000 a month for all debts. Put it all toward principal and interest and that's a loan of roughly $443,000. Taxes, insurance, HOA dues and car payments come out of that same budget.

Data note: Rates are Freddie Mac's Primary Mortgage Market Survey for the week of Nov. 5, 2026. DTI limits are from Fannie Mae's Selling Guide B3-6-02 and the CFPB. Prices are Phoenix REALTORS/ShowingTime Local Market Updates for October 2026. Payment math is standard 30-year amortization at 7.18%, calculated November 10, 2026. Your lender's numbers will differ.

Today's Numbers at a Glance

  • 30-year fixed: 7.18%, down from 7.24% a week earlier (Freddie Mac)
  • 15-year fixed: 6.57%
  • Principal and interest per $100,000: about $677/month at 7.18%
  • Phoenix median single-family price (Oct. 2026): $481,998
  • Scottsdale median single-family price (Oct. 2026): $1,225,000
  • Scottsdale median townhouse/condo (Oct. 2026): $485,000
  • Phoenix median townhouse/condo (Oct. 2026): $320,000

Step 1: Find Your DTI Budget

The CFPB defines DTI as all monthly debt payments divided by gross monthly income. Lenders count your future housing payment (principal, interest, taxes, insurance, HOA) plus car loans, student loans, card minimums and similar debts.

  • $100,000 salary: $8,333/month gross; $3,000 at 36% DTI; $3,750 at 45%
  • $150,000 salary: $12,500/month; $4,500 at 36%; $5,625 at 45%
  • $200,000 salary: $16,667/month; $6,000 at 36%; $7,500 at 45%

Step 2: Turn the Budget Into a Loan Amount

If the full 36% budget went to principal and interest at 7.18%, with no other debts:

  • $100,000 salary: about $443,000 loan
  • $150,000 salary: about $664,000
  • $200,000 salary: about $886,000

These are ceilings, not targets. In real life, property taxes, homeowners insurance and HOA dues come out of the same budget, so your approved loan will be lower. For premiums, see our guide to homeowners insurance costs in Arizona.

Step 3: Compare With Local Prices

With 20% down, principal and interest at 7.18%:

  • Phoenix single-family ($481,998): $385,598 loan, about $2,612/month. Keeping that within 36% takes about $87,000 in gross income
  • Scottsdale townhouse/condo ($485,000): $388,000 loan, about $2,628/month. About $88,000 in income
  • Phoenix townhouse/condo ($320,000): $256,000 loan, about $1,734/month. About $58,000 in income
  • Scottsdale single-family ($1,225,000): $980,000 loan, about $6,639/month. About $221,000 in income. That loan is above Maricopa County's 2026 conforming limit, so it's a jumbo loan. See our jumbo loan limit guide

Again, add taxes, insurance and HOA dues to every line.

Ways to Stretch Your Budget

  • Bigger down payment: each $100,000 you don't borrow saves about $677/month at 7.18%
  • Pay down other debts before applying to free up DTI room

Frequently Asked Questions

How much house can I afford with a $100,000 salary in Arizona?

At 36% DTI, about $3,000/month for all debts. That's a loan of about $443,000 if all of it went to principal and interest at 7.18%, less after taxes and insurance.

What income do you need to buy a house in Phoenix?

About $87,000 to keep principal and interest on the $481,998 median (20% down) within 36%, before taxes and insurance.

What debt-to-income ratio do lenders allow?

Fannie Mae: 36% manual, up to 45% with compensating factors, up to 50% via Desktop Underwriter.

How much is a mortgage payment on $400,000 in Arizona?

About $2,710/month in principal and interest at 7.18%, plus taxes and insurance.
