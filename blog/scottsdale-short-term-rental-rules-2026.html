Scottsdale Short-Term Rental Rules 2026: Licenses, Ordinance 4720 & What Buyers Should Know

Do you need a license for a short-term rental in Scottsdale? Yes. Any Scottsdale home rented for less than 30 days needs an annual city license, at $250 per property, on top of an Arizona TPT license, $500,000 in liability coverage, neighbor notification and Maricopa County registration. Operating without one carries a fine of at least $1,000 per violation.

What changed in 2026? In August, the City Council voted 7–0 for Ordinance No. 4720. Each rental's designated emergency contact must now be reachable 24/7 by phone or email for complaints, such as noise or parking, not just emergencies. If you're buying a Scottsdale home with rental income in mind, or living next to one, here's the full picture.

Data note: Requirements are from the City of Scottsdale's "Information for Owners and Operators" page and Scottsdale City Code Article IX. Ordinance 4720 details are from the Scottsdale mayor's office, Scottsdale Progress/scottsdale.org coverage of the August 24, 2026 council meeting, and Citizen Portal. All read October 21, 2026.

Scottsdale STR Requirements: The Five Steps

Step 1: Arizona TPT License

  • Required for each rental property, from the Arizona Department of Revenue
  • Needed first: your TPT number goes on the city application

Step 2: Scottsdale License

  • Annual fee: $250 per property
  • Applies to: rentals of less than 30 days only; 30-day-plus rentals don't need this license
  • Legal basis: Ordinance 4566, effective January 8, 2023

Step 3: Neighbor Notice and Insurance

  • Within 30 days of licensing, or whenever contact information changes
  • Who gets notice: single-family properties adjacent to, directly across and diagonally across the street; in multifamily buildings, all units on the same floor
  • What it says: license number, rental address, and the 24-hour emergency contact's name, address and phone
  • Liability coverage: at least $500,000, directly or through the booking platform

Step 4: Maricopa County Registration

  • Register with the County Assessor under A.R.S. 33-1902 before the property is occupied

Step 5: Post the Required Notice

  • Where: inside the front door and the primary door to the backyard, or a conspicuous spot near each
  • Format: 14-point or larger bold type, laminated or similarly protected
  • Contents include: locations of fire and life safety equipment and city waste rules

What Ordinance 4720 Changed

  • Before: the designated contact had to handle emergencies
  • Now: the same contact must be reachable 24/7 by phone or email for complaints and emergencies
  • Still required: an in-person response within one hour when public safety personnel request it during an emergency
  • Not required: an in-person response to complaints, or instantly resolving them
  • Also added: a definition of "event center"
  • Penalty: Scottsdale Progress reported fines starting at $500 for owners who don't respond

Why Scottsdale Is Tightening the Rules

The city attorney told the council the measure "closes a loophole." Sgt. Eric Bolles of the Scottsdale Police Department's short-term rentals unit described cases where officers cleared a party, then wrote to the owner or contact and got no response. The mayor's office framed it as making sure complaints don't go unanswered.

Other Rules That Apply

  • Pool barriers: short-term rentals with a pool, hot tub or spa must meet city pool barrier requirements
  • Nuisance parties: SRC 18-121 holds owners accountable for repeated unruly gatherings at any private residence
  • State law limits: A.R.S. 9-500.39 limits city authority; broader changes generally need the Legislature
  • What's next: Scottsdale backed a League of Arizona Cities and Towns proposal, now headed to the 2027 session, to bar multifamily properties, specifically including condominiums, from operating as short-term rentals

That last item is a watch-list issue for anyone buying a Scottsdale condo with nightly rental income in the plan.

FAQ: Scottsdale Short-Term Rentals

Do you need a license for a short-term rental in Scottsdale?

Yes, for stays under 30 days: $250 per property per year, plus a state TPT license.

Why is Scottsdale tightening short-term rental rules?

To speed up responses to complaints. Ordinance 4720 requires 24/7 reachable contacts.

What are Scottsdale's short-term rental requirements?

TPT license, city license, neighbor notice, $500,000 liability coverage, county registration and a posted notice.

Can Scottsdale ban short-term rentals?

Not by itself; A.R.S. 9-500.39 limits cities. A 2027 legislative proposal targets condos and other multifamily.
