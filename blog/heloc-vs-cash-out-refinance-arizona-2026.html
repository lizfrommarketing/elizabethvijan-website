HELOC vs. Cash-Out Refinance in Arizona: Which Makes Sense With Rates Above 7%?

HELOC or cash-out refinance? If your current mortgage rate is well below today's market, a HELOC usually lets you tap equity without giving that rate up. Freddie Mac's survey put the average 30-year fixed at 7.32% as of Oct. 22, 2026, up from 6.19% a year earlier. A cash-out refinance replaces your whole mortgage at the new rate.

When does a cash-out refi win? When your current rate is at or above today's, when you want one fixed payment, or when you need a large sum and value rate certainty over flexibility. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) notes cash-out refis generally carry higher closing costs.

Data note: Rates come from Freddie Mac's Primary Mortgage Market Survey (week of Oct. 22, 2026). Product features come from the CFPB's HELOC guide. Tax treatment comes from IRS Publication 936. Not a loan offer; terms vary by lender.

The Rate Picture This Week

Freddie Mac PMMS, as of Oct. 22, 2026:

  • 30-year fixed: 7.32%, down from 7.40% the prior week
  • 30-year fixed a year ago: 6.19%
  • 15-year fixed: 6.68%

If you bought or refinanced when rates were lower, a cash-out refi would reset your entire balance at today's level. For more on where rates are heading, see are mortgage rates going down?

How a HELOC Works

The Basics (CFPB)

  • An open-end line of credit you can borrow from repeatedly, up to a limit
  • Secured by your home equity: value minus what you owe
  • Usually a variable rate; some plans let you convert part of the balance to a fixed rate
  • Draw period to borrow, then a repayment period; some plans end in a balloon payment

The Fine Print

  • Fees may include an appraisal, application, closing costs and sometimes annual or inactivity fees
  • Right to cancel: under federal law you can cancel within three days after the account is opened and get your fees back
  • Lenders can generally freeze or reduce your line if your home's value falls or your finances change
  • Renting your home may be prohibited under some HELOC terms
  • If you sell, you're generally required to pay off the HELOC in full

How a Cash-Out Refinance Works

  • You replace your existing mortgage with a larger one and take the difference in cash
  • One payment, fixed or variable
  • CFPB: closing costs are generally higher, and the new rate may be higher than your current mortgage
  • Can make sense if your current rate is near or above today's market, or if you want to consolidate a first mortgage and existing HELOC into one fixed loan

Side-by-Side

Choose a HELOC If

  • Your first mortgage rate is meaningfully lower than today's
  • You need money over time (a phased remodel, a pool, tuition)
  • You can handle a variable payment
  • You want lower upfront costs

Choose a Cash-Out Refi If

  • Your current rate is at or above the market
  • You need a large lump sum and want a fixed payment
  • You plan to stay put long enough to recover closing costs

Consider a Home Equity Loan If

  • You want a lump sum and a fixed rate without touching your first mortgage

Taxes and Equity Uses

  • IRS Publication 936: interest is deductible as home mortgage interest only to the extent proceeds are used to buy, build or substantially improve the home securing the loan
  • Remodel = possibly deductible; credit card payoff or a car = not
  • Improvements may also add to your basis, which matters when you sell; see our Arizona capital gains guide

Questions to Ask Arizona Lenders

  • What index and margin set my HELOC rate, and what are the caps?
  • How long are the draw and repayment periods?
  • Is there a balloon payment?
  • What are the closing costs and any early termination fee?
  • Can I convert part of the balance to a fixed rate?
  • The CFPB suggests getting at least three HELOC estimates

FAQ: HELOC vs. Cash-Out Refinance

Is a HELOC or a cash-out refinance better right now?

If your current rate is below 7.32%, a HELOC usually preserves it; a cash-out refi replaces it.

What is the difference between a HELOC and a home equity loan?

A HELOC is a revolving line, usually variable; a home equity loan is a lump sum, typically fixed.

Is HELOC interest tax deductible?

Only if used to buy, build or substantially improve the home, per IRS Pub. 936.

Do you have to pay off a HELOC when you sell your house?

Generally, yes, in full, from your sale proceeds.
