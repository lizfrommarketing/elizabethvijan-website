Relocating to Scottsdale or Phoenix: A Newcomer's Guide

Elizabeth has helped clients relocate to Scottsdale and Phoenix from across the country, including buyers moving sight-unseen. Here's what tends to matter most to newcomers.

Plan Around the Climate

Summers here are genuinely hot, with temperatures regularly exceeding 100 degrees from June through September, while winters are mild and sunny — the reason so many buyers arrive as seasonal residents before making the move permanent. If possible, plan your house-hunting trip for the cooler months, when it's easiest to evaluate a neighborhood's outdoor lifestyle honestly.

Understand That "Scottsdale" and "Phoenix" Aren't One Market

Newcomers often search generically for "Scottsdale" or "Phoenix" homes without realizing how different the sub-markets are:

  • North Scottsdale: Golf-anchored, guard-gated, resort-adjacent
  • Chaparral Park / 85250: Central, walkable, park-side
  • Arcadia & Arcadia Lite: Historic ranch character, canal-and-mountain geography, destination dining
  • Central Phoenix: Historic districts, urban proximity, architectural variety

Getting specific about lifestyle priorities early narrows the search dramatically.

You Can Buy Remotely — With the Right Support

Out-of-state buyers regularly purchase homes here without an in-person visit before closing, relying on a local agent for video walkthroughs, honest neighborhood context, and coordination through inspection and closing. It's not the norm for every buyer, but it's a well-established path for the right situation.

Factor in Commute and Airport Access

Sky Harbor International Airport sits centrally enough that most Central Phoenix and Arcadia-area neighborhoods offer a short drive, while North Scottsdale's more remote communities mean a longer commute both to the airport and to central business districts — worth weighing if frequent travel or a downtown commute is part of your routine.

Talk to Someone Who Knows the Details

HOA structures, historic district guidelines, golf club membership requirements, and school boundaries all vary block by block in this market. A conversation with a local agent before you start touring can save weeks of searching in the wrong neighborhoods.

What is the weather like in Scottsdale and Phoenix?

Scottsdale and Phoenix have a desert climate with hot summers, often exceeding 100 degrees Fahrenheit from June through September, and mild, sunny winters that draw seasonal residents from across the country. Many newcomers plan their move and house-hunting trips around the cooler months.

Can I buy a home in Scottsdale or Phoenix without visiting in person first?

Yes. Many out-of-state buyers purchase remotely with the help of a local agent who can provide video walkthroughs, detailed neighborhood context, and guidance through inspection and closing, though an in-person visit before or shortly after closing is still recommended when possible.

Which Scottsdale or Phoenix neighborhood is right for someone relocating?

It depends on lifestyle priorities. Buyers wanting walkability and historic character often look at Central Phoenix's Willo or Encanto-Palmcroft districts; those wanting golf and resort-style living look toward North Scottsdale; and buyers wanting a central, dining-forward location often consider Arcadia, Arcadia Lite, or Scottsdale's Chaparral Park area.
