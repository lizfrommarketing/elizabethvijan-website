How to Make an Offer on a House in Arizona: Price, Earnest Money, Contingencies and What Happens After You Sign

How do you make an offer on a house in Arizona? Your agent writes it on the Arizona REALTORS® Residential Resale Real Estate Purchase Contract, with your price, earnest money, loan terms, closing date and contingencies, then delivers it with a deadline for the seller to respond. The seller can accept, counter or reject. Until the seller accepts and delivers the signed contract, you can withdraw.

How much to offer: start from comparable sales. In October 2026, Scottsdale single-family sellers received 96.8% of list price on average and Phoenix sellers 97.7%, with homes taking 75 and 65 days to sell. That's a market with room to negotiate.

Step 1: Get Ready Before You Find the House

  • Pre-approval letter: sellers expect one with a financed offer. See how long a pre-approval lasts
  • Proof of funds: for your down payment and closing costs
  • Your agreement with your agent: since Aug. 17, 2024, agents who use the MLS must have a written buyer agreement signed before touring a home with you. Our guide to buyer broker agreements in Arizona explains it
  • Your rate: Freddie Mac's 30-year average was 7.21% on Nov. 25, 2026; know your payment at your top price

Step 2: Decide on Price

Your agent should pull recent sales of similar homes nearby. Then weigh the market:

October 2026 benchmarks (single-family)

  • Scottsdale: median $1,225,000; 96.8% of list received; 75 days on market; 4.2 months of supply
  • Phoenix: median $481,998; 97.7% of list received; 65 days on market; 4.2 months of supply

Signals you can offer below list

  • The home has been on the market well past the local median days on market
  • There's been a price reduction
  • Similar homes nearby are sitting unsold

Signals to offer closer to list

  • New listing in a tight price band, or multiple offers expected
  • Similar homes nearby went under contract quickly. (Citywide, Scottsdale condos had 6.6 months of supply, so quick condo sales are less common right now)

Step 3: Choose Your Terms

Price is one lever. Terms are the rest.

  • Earnest money: the contract leaves the amount blank to negotiate; it's deposited with escrow or a broker's trust account and credited to you at closing. See how much earnest money in Arizona
  • Close of escrow date: must be a specific date; escrow closes when the deed records with the county
  • Seller concessions: a credit toward closing costs or a rate buydown can be worth more than a price cut. Compare in rate buydown vs. price reduction
  • What's included: fixtures and listed personal property; check what stays with the house

Step 4: Keep Your Protections

Under the Arizona REALTORS® resale contract:

  • Inspection period: 10 days by default. Within it, you can cancel or ask the seller for repairs
  • Seller disclosures: the Phoenix REALTORS® sample contract has the seller deliver the SPDS within 3 days after acceptance and a 5-year insurance claims history within 5 days
  • Appraisal contingency: if the appraisal comes in below the price, you have 5 days after notice to cancel and get your earnest money back
  • Loan contingency: deliver notice of no loan approval at least 3 days before close of escrow to keep your earnest money. Not locking a rate isn't an unfulfilled loan contingency

Waiving protections can make an offer stronger, but it shifts risk to you. See can I back out of buying a house?

Step 5: Set an Expiration, Then Wait

The sample contract's Terms of Acceptance say the offer expires unless the seller's written acceptance is delivered by the date and time you fill in, Arizona time. If no acceptance arrives, the offer is withdrawn and your earnest money is returned. Until the seller accepts and delivers the fully executed agreement, you may withdraw at any time.

Step 6: Accept, Counter or Walk

The seller's response page has three boxes:

  • Acceptance: you're in contract, and the clock starts on your inspection period. Days are calendar days
  • Counteroffer: the seller accepts your terms with listed changes. You can accept, counter back or let it expire
  • Rejection: the offer is off the table

After you're under contract, the seller may still accept other offers, but only as backups.

“The best offers I write aren't always the highest. They're the cleanest: a strong pre-approval, a realistic closing date and an inspection period the seller can live with. In this market, I also ask for a seller credit far more often than a lower price, because a buydown changes the monthly payment, and that's what my buyers actually feel.”

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you make an offer on a house in Arizona?

Your agent writes it on the AAR resale contract, signs it with you and delivers it with a deadline.

How much should I offer on a house?

Base it on comparable sales. October sellers received about 97% of list.

How long does a seller have to respond to an offer in Arizona?

Until the expiration you set in the offer.

Can I back out after making an offer in Arizona?

Before acceptance, yes. After, through contingencies like the 10-day inspection period.
