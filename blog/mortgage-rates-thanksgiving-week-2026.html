Mortgage Rates Thanksgiving Week 2026: Where Rates Stand, What They Mean for Phoenix and Scottsdale Payments, and How to Handle a Holiday Closing

What are mortgage rates this week? Freddie Mac's 30-year fixed-rate mortgage averaged 7.28% as of Nov. 19, 2026, up from 7.11% the week before. The 15-year fixed averaged 6.66%. A year ago, the 30-year stood at 6.26%.

What it means in dollars: at Phoenix's October median price with 20% down, principal and interest runs about $2,638 a month at today's average, roughly $261 more than at last year's rate. Forecasts don't point to a quick drop, so the payment you can carry today matters more than timing the market.

Data note: Rates are from Freddie Mac's Primary Mortgage Market Survey (PMMS), Nov. 19, 2026 release. The rate forecast is from Fannie Mae's November 2026 Housing Forecast. Prices are from Phoenix REALTORS® Local Market Updates for Phoenix and Scottsdale, October 2026 (latest published). Payment math is our arithmetic. Read November 25, 2026.

This Week's Numbers

From Freddie Mac's PMMS (Nov. 19, 2026):

  • 30-year fixed: 7.28%, up from 7.11% a week earlier
  • 15-year fixed: 6.66%, up from 6.45%
  • 30-year, a year ago: 6.26%
  • 15-year, a year ago: 5.54%

PMMS reflects loans submitted to Freddie Mac by lenders for borrowers with good to excellent credit putting 20% down on a home purchase. Your quote can be higher or lower based on credit, down payment, loan type and points.

The Trend Into the Holidays

Rates have spent the fall moving higher. The 30-year averaged 7.11% on Nov. 12 and 7.28% on Nov. 19, and today's 7.28% compares with 6.26% a year ago.

Looking ahead:

  • Fannie Mae's November 2026 forecast, based on rates as of Oct. 30, projects the 30-year averaging 7.2% in every quarter of 2027
  • The Federal Reserve meets Dec. 8-9, 2026; mortgage rates often react to its statement and outlook, though they don't move in lockstep with the Fed's rate

Forecasts get revised monthly. Treat them as a planning range, not a promise.

What It Means for Valley Payments

Principal and interest only, 30-year fixed, 20% down, at the October 2026 medians:

Phoenix (median $481,998; loan about $385,600)

  • At 7.28% (this week): about $2,638/month
  • At 6.26% (a year ago): about $2,377/month

Scottsdale (median $1,225,000; loan $980,000)

  • At 7.28%: about $6,705/month
  • At 6.26%: about $6,040/month

A Scottsdale loan that size is above the 2026 conforming limit of $832,750 for a one-unit home in Maricopa County, so it would be a jumbo loan, which is priced separately from the PMMS average. See jumbo loan limits in Arizona. Property taxes, insurance and HOA dues come on top.

Closing Over Thanksgiving: A Checklist

If your closing date lands this week or next, a few things deserve attention now:

  • Check your rate lock's expiration. If it ends over the long weekend, ask your lender today what an extension costs. More in how long you can lock a mortgage rate
  • Confirm holiday hours for your lender, title and escrow company. Many close or shorten hours on Thanksgiving and the Friday after
  • Send funds early. Wires can't settle on a bank holiday, and Thursday is one
  • Plan the signing. Get signing appointments on the calendar before the office closes for the holiday
  • Keep documents current. Pay stubs and bank statements can age out if a closing slips into December

Weighing a December closing against January? See should I close on a house in December or January.

Buyers: Is Waiting Worth It?

The local market gives buyers some room. In Phoenix in October, pending sales fell 48.2% from a year earlier and inventory rose 14.3% to 3,380 homes, while sellers received 97.7% of list price. In Scottsdale, pending sales fell 43.5% and months of supply sat at 4.2. More choice and slower contract activity can mean more negotiating room, including seller-paid buydowns.

“The question I hear most this week isn't 'what's the rate?' It's 'should I wait?' My answer is to run the payment at today's rate and make sure it works for your budget. If rates fall later, refinancing is an option. A home that fits your life at today's payment is worth more than a forecast.”
— Elizabeth Vijan, Tackett Team, eXp Realty
