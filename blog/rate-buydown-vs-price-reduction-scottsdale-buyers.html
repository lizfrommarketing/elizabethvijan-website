Rate Buydown vs. Price Reduction: How Scottsdale Buyers Should Choose in 2026

Is a mortgage rate buydown better than a price reduction in Scottsdale? Often, yes, if your main challenge is the monthly payment. A seller-paid buydown puts money directly toward your interest rate, which can lower your payment more than the same dollars taken off the price. A price reduction is the better choice if you need a smaller down payment, a smaller loan or protection against a low appraisal, or if you expect to refinance soon.

There is no universal winner. The right answer depends on three things: how long you'll keep the loan, how much cash you have and what your lender's pricing looks like on the day you lock. Run both scenarios with your lender before you negotiate.

Your Three Options, Explained

Option 1: Temporary Buydown (2-1 or 3-2-1)

The seller, or sometimes the builder, prepays the cost of lowering your rate for the first few years.

  • 2-1 buydown: Your rate is two percentage points below the note rate in year one, one point below in year two, then the full rate from year three on.
  • 3-2-1 buydown: Three points below in year one, two in year two, one in year three, then the full rate.
  • Best for: Buyers who expect income growth or who may refinance if rates fall.
  • Watch out for: The payment steps up on schedule. Many lenders qualify you at the full note rate, so confirm how yours qualifies you, and ask what happens to any unused buydown funds if you refinance or sell early.

Option 2: Permanent Buydown (Discount Points)

Points paid at closing lower your rate for the life of the loan.

  • How it works: Each point costs a percentage of the loan amount, and your lender sets how much rate reduction each point buys.
  • Best for: Buyers who plan to keep the loan for many years.
  • Watch out for: The break-even point. If you refinance or sell before you've recovered the cost in monthly savings, the money is largely lost.

Option 3: Price Reduction

The seller lowers the contract price.

  • How it works: A lower price means a smaller loan and, if your down payment is a percentage of the price, less cash at closing.
  • Best for: Buyers who are short on cash, worried about the appraisal, or likely to refinance soon.
  • Watch out for: On its own, a modest price cut usually moves the monthly payment less than buyers expect.

A Hypothetical Example (Not a Real Quote)

This is a hypothetical example to show the logic. It is not based on a real listing or a current rate quote.

Say a seller agrees to give $20,000.

  • As a price reduction: With 20% down, your loan shrinks by $16,000, and you bring $4,000 less to closing. Your payment drops modestly and permanently.
  • As a temporary buydown: The $20,000 is put toward lowering your payment in the first year or two. Your lender can tell you how far it goes on your loan size.
  • As a permanent buydown: The $20,000 buys discount points. Your lender can tell you the rate reduction and the break-even timeline.

Your lender can price all three on the same loan, on the same day. Compare the monthly payment, the cash to close and the total cost over the years you expect to keep the loan.

How I Help Clients Decide

  • Ask the timeline question first. How long will you realistically keep this loan?
  • Get a side-by-side from your lender: price cut vs. temporary buydown vs. permanent buydown.

FAQ: Buydowns vs. Price Reductions

Is a mortgage rate buydown better than a price reduction in Scottsdale?

It depends on your goal. Buydowns usually help the monthly payment more, while price cuts lower your loan and down payment. Compare both with your lender using today's rates.

What is a 2-1 buydown?

A temporary buydown: two points off your rate in year one, one point off in year two, then the full rate.

Is a permanent buydown worth it?

It can be, if you keep the loan past the break-even point. Your lender can calculate it for you.

Can the seller pay for my buydown?

Often, yes, as a negotiated concession within your loan program's contribution limits.
