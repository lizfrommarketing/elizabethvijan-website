Is It a Good Time to Buy a House in Arizona? Rates, Prices and Buyer Leverage Heading Into December 2026

Is it a good time to buy a house in Arizona? For buyers with steady income who plan to stay put, late 2026 offers something the last few years didn't: leverage. Phoenix had 14.3% more single-family homes for sale in October than a year earlier, sellers received 97.7% of list price on average, and pending sales were down sharply. The cost is rates: Freddie Mac's 30-year average was 7.21% on Nov. 25, 2026, down from 7.28% a week earlier but up from 6.23% a year ago.

The short version: prices are holding, inventory is up, and competition is down. You'll pay more in interest than a 2025 buyer did, but you can negotiate harder on price and terms.

Data note: Rates are from Freddie Mac's Primary Mortgage Market Survey (PMMS), Nov. 25, 2026 release, published a day early for Thanksgiving. The rate forecast is from Fannie Mae's November 2026 Housing Forecast. Market figures are from Phoenix REALTORS® Local Market Updates for Phoenix and Scottsdale, October 2026, the latest published. Payment math is our arithmetic. Read November 26, 2026.

Rates: Where They Stand

Freddie Mac PMMS, week of Nov. 25, 2026:

  • 30-year fixed: 7.21%, down from 7.28% last week; 6.23% a year ago
  • 15-year fixed: 6.54%, down from 6.66% last week; 5.51% a year ago

Looking ahead: Fannie Mae's November forecast, based on rates as of Oct. 30, projects the 30-year averaging 7.2% in every quarter of 2027. Forecasts change, but that's a reason not to build your plan around a big drop.

Prices and Inventory: October 2026

Phoenix (single-family)

  • Median sales price: $481,998, up 2.6%
  • Inventory: 3,380 homes, up 14.3%
  • Months of supply: 4.2, up from 3.6
  • Pending sales: 427, down from 825
  • Days on market: 65
  • Percent of list price received: 97.7%

Scottsdale (single-family)

  • Median sales price: $1,225,000, up 5.6%
  • Inventory: 1,480 homes
  • Months of supply: 4.2
  • Percent of list price received: 96.8%

Phoenix townhouses and condos

  • Median: $320,000, with 6.7 months of supply, the most room to negotiate of the three

What the Monthly Payment Looks Like

Principal and interest only, 20% down, at the Phoenix October median of $481,998 (a $385,598 loan):

  • 30-year at 7.21%: about $2,620 a month
  • Same loan at last year's 6.23%: about $2,369
  • 15-year at 6.54%: about $3,367

The gap between today's rate and last year's is about $250 a month on that loan. Taxes, insurance and any HOA come on top. A seller credit toward a rate buydown can close part of that gap; we compare the options in rate buydown vs. price reduction.

Reasons Buying Now Can Make Sense

  • Negotiating room: more inventory and fewer pending sales mean sellers are more open to credits, repairs and price cuts
  • Less competition: with pending sales down by about half in Phoenix, multiple-offer situations are less common
  • Prices aren't falling citywide: waiting for a crash means betting against the latest data
  • Refinancing is an option later: if rates fall, you may be able to refinance, though that has costs and isn't guaranteed

Reasons to Wait

  • Your job or plans are uncertain: buying and selling within a few years rarely pays after transaction costs
  • Your savings are thin: closing costs and reserves matter as much as the down payment. See how much house you can afford
  • You need the lowest payment possible: at 7.21%, renting a bit longer may fit your budget better. We run the numbers in rent or buy in Arizona

Is December a Good Month to Buy?

Holiday listings are fewer, but sellers who stay on the market through December are often motivated. If you're comparing year-end and January closings for tax or budget reasons, read should I close in December or January?

“I tell buyers to stop trying to time rates and start using the leverage they have. Right now that means asking for seller credits, a longer inspection window or a price that reflects days on market. You can change a rate later. You can't renegotiate the price after closing.”
— Elizabeth Vijan, Tackett Team, eXp Realty

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it a good time to buy a house in Arizona right now?

For stable, long-term buyers, yes: more inventory and less competition, at higher rates.

Will home prices drop in Arizona?

No one can promise that. October medians rose 2.6% in Phoenix and 5.6% in Scottsdale.

Is December a good time to buy a house?

It can be. Fewer listings, but motivated sellers.

What are mortgage rates in Arizona today?

Freddie Mac's national 30-year average was 7.21% on Nov. 25, 2026.
