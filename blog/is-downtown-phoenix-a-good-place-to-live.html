Is Downtown Phoenix a Good Place to Live? A 2026 Guide to Living Downtown

Is downtown Phoenix a good place to live? If you want an urban, walk-to-everything routine in the Valley, downtown Phoenix is the strongest option it has. About 25,118 residents now live in the Downtown Phoenix Inc. service area, up from 9,051 in 2000. The core has 255+ restaurants and bars, a 55,000-square-foot Fry's, METRO light rail, and home venues for the Diamondbacks, Suns and Mercury.

It also comes with trade-offs: event-night crowds, summer heat that shapes how far you'll actually walk, an office market that is still filling up, and condo HOA budgets you need to read closely. The best fit comes from matching a specific building and block to your daily routine.

Data note: Neighborhood figures come from Downtown Phoenix Inc.'s Facts + Figures (August 2026). Project news is from Axios Phoenix, azfamily and Newmark (October 2026). Housing figures are Phoenix REALTORS/ShowingTime September 2026 data for the City of Phoenix, not downtown alone.

Downtown Phoenix by the Numbers

People and Housing

  • Population: 25,118 today; 19,520 in 2020; 9,051 in 2000
  • Residential units built since 2000: 15,375 (11,014 since 2015)
  • Under construction: 1,027 units
  • Average stabilized occupancy: 93%

Daily Life

  • Restaurants and bars: 255+, nearly 80% locally owned
  • Grocery: a 55,000-sq.-ft. Fry's
  • Parks: 7 downtown parks
  • Transit: METRO light rail since December 2008, 8 more miles of track completed in 2025, and 25+ routes serving downtown
  • Airport: about 10 minutes to Sky Harbor, per Downtown Phoenix Inc.

Culture and Campus

  • Sports: Diamondbacks, Suns and Mercury all play downtown
  • Arts: the Orpheum Theatre, Symphony Hall, the Herberger Theater Center, and First Friday on Roosevelt Row
  • Education: about 18,000 students at ASU's Downtown Phoenix campus, plus the University of Arizona College of Medicine–Phoenix in the Phoenix Bioscience Core

What's New This Month

A $60 Million YMCA Returns Downtown

On October 19, the Suns, Mercury and Valley of the Sun YMCA announced a $60 million YMCA on the site of the Lincoln Family Downtown YMCA, near 1st Avenue and Polk Street. That building opened in 1952 and closed last month.

  • Size: three stories, 55,000 sq. ft.
  • Courts: one NBA-size court and two high-school-size courts
  • Programs: tutoring, internships, counseling, career training, nutrition classes and a café
  • Housing: VanTrust Real Estate plans about 60 affordable units in three stories above the center
  • Timeline: groundbreaking February 18, 2027, during NBA All-Star week in Phoenix (per Axios), with opening targeted for 2029

333 Tower Adds a Major Tenant and Plans a Rooftop

Trinity Capital signed a long-term lease for the top 2½ floors of 333 Tower at 333 N. Central Ave. Owner Foundation 8 plans a rooftop restaurant and bar and a ground-floor café. The rooftop operator hasn't been named yet.

Residential Projects in the Pipeline

  • The Henri: 313 units (2026)
  • Greenprint on 3rd: 77 units (2026)
  • The Moreland: 237 units (2027)
  • The Whitney: 328 units (2028)

The Trade-Offs to Weigh

  • Office vacancy is 18.9% in the downtown business core, per Downtown Phoenix Inc. Some blocks are busier than others on weekdays.
  • Event nights change traffic and parking. Check a building's garage access on game and concert nights.
  • Heat is real. Shade, covered parking and the walk to light rail matter more in July than on a map.
  • Construction continues. A view or a quiet street can change as new towers rise. Ask what's planned on adjacent lots.
  • HOA budgets vary widely in high-rise buildings. Review reserves, special assessments and what the dues cover.

What Housing Costs Look Like

Phoenix REALTORS/ShowingTime, City of Phoenix, September 2026:

  • Townhouse/condo median: $329,000 (+2.8% year over year), 92 median days on market, 6.4 months of supply
  • Single-family median: $485,000 (−3.5%), 68 median days on market, 3.9 months of supply

These are citywide figures. Downtown high-rises, lofts and new construction often price above the condo median, while historic districts nearby carry their own premiums. FAQ: Living in Downtown Phoenix

Is downtown Phoenix growing?

Yes: 25,118 residents today versus 19,520 in 2020, with 1,027 units under construction.

Is downtown Phoenix walkable?

The 1.7-square-mile core packs in restaurants, a grocery store, parks, sports venues and light rail. Heat, not distance, is the usual limit.

What is being built in downtown Phoenix?

The Henri, The Moreland and The Whitney, plus the new $60 million YMCA with about 60 affordable homes (opening targeted for 2029).

How much are condos in Phoenix right now?

The citywide median was $329,000 in September 2026. Downtown towers and new builds often run higher.
