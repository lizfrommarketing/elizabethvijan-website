Is It Better to Rent or Buy a House in Arizona Right Now? November 2026 Rents, Prices and Payments Compared

Is it better to rent or buy right now? On monthly cost alone, renting is cheaper across most of the Valley today: Zillow's typical Phoenix rent was about $1,568 in October 2026, while principal and interest on a median-priced Phoenix home is roughly $2,968 with 10% down at this week's 7.28% average rate. Buying makes more sense the longer you plan to stay, the more you value control over your home, and the more you want each payment building equity.

Why the gap is wide right now: Freddie Mac's 30-year fixed average rose to 7.28% on November 19, 2026, up from 6.26% a year ago, while Valley rents have risen only modestly.

Data note: Rents are from the Zillow Observed Rent Index (ZORI, all home types, smoothed) for October 2026. Home prices are from Phoenix REALTORS/ShowingTime October 2026 Local Market Updates (ARMLS data, current as of November 3, 2026). Rates are from Freddie Mac's PMMS for November 19, 2026. Payment math is principal and interest only. Read November 20, 2026.

Rents: What Zillow Shows

  • Phoenix: about $1,568 a month, up about 1.4% year over year
  • Scottsdale: about $2,142, up about 3.1%
  • Phoenix metro: about $1,717, up about 1.4%

ZORI tracks typical asking rents across houses, townhomes and apartments, so a specific single-family rental can run higher.

Buying: Prices and Payments

Phoenix single-family, October 2026

  • Median sales price: $481,998
  • With 10% down (loan about $433,800) at 7.28%: about $2,968 a month principal and interest
  • First-year principal paid down: about $4,200; interest about $31,400

Scottsdale townhouse/condo, October 2026

  • Median sales price: $485,000
  • With 20% down (loan $388,000) at 7.28%: about $2,655 a month principal and interest

Scottsdale single-family, October 2026

  • Median sales price: $1,225,000, well into jumbo loan territory for most buyers

Add the extras: property taxes, homeowners insurance, HOA dues, maintenance, and with less than 20% down, mortgage insurance. Renters typically pay renter's insurance and utilities but not repairs.

The Case for Renting Right Now

  • Lower monthly cost in most Valley comparisons above
  • Flexibility if your job, family or timeline may change in the next few years
  • No closing costs or selling costs; see closing costs for buyers in Arizona
  • Time to save a larger down payment, which lowers the payment and can remove mortgage insurance

The Case for Buying Right Now

  • Equity: every payment pays down some principal, and you own any appreciation
  • Stability: a fixed-rate payment doesn't rise with rents
  • Negotiating room: Phoenix sellers received 97.7% of list price in October and inventory was up 14.3% from a year earlier, giving buyers more choices than in recent years
  • Refinance option: if rates fall later, you may be able to refinance, though it's never guaranteed

Five Questions That Decide It for You

  1. How long will you stay? The longer, the more buying spreads out one-time costs
  2. Can you cover the full monthly cost of owning, not just principal and interest?
  3. Do you have reserves after the down payment and closing costs?
  4. What would you rent instead? Compare a similar home, not the metro average
  5. How much do you value control: pets, remodels, a yard, no lease renewals?

For a bigger-picture view of qualifying, see how much house you can afford in Arizona.

If You Rent for Now, Make It Count

  • Set a savings target for down payment and closing costs, and automate it
  • Check your credit and pay down revolving balances; see what credit score you need
  • Watch the market in the neighborhoods you'd buy in, not just headlines
  • Time your lease so it ends when you'd realistically be ready to close

Renting strategically can put you in a stronger position, with a bigger down payment and a clearer budget, when the right home comes along.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it better to rent or buy a house right now in Arizona?

Renting is cheaper monthly in most comparisons today; buying can win for longer stays.

How much is rent in Phoenix and Scottsdale?

About $1,568 in Phoenix and $2,142 in Scottsdale in October 2026, per Zillow's ZORI.

Is it better to rent or buy a condo?

In Scottsdale, a median condo's principal and interest is about $2,655 with 20% down, before HOA and taxes, versus about $2,142 typical rent.

How long should you stay in a house to make buying worth it?

It depends on your costs and the market; the longer you stay, the better buying tends to look.
