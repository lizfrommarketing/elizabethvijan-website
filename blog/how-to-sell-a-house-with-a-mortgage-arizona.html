How to Sell a House With a Mortgage in Arizona: Payoff Statements, Net Proceeds and What Happens at Closing

Can you sell a house with a mortgage? Yes, and most sellers do: your loan is paid off from the sale proceeds at closing. The escrow or title company requests a payoff statement from your servicer, wires that amount to the lender on closing day, pays your costs and sends you the rest. Your lender then releases its lien on the property.

The number to know is your payoff, not your balance. The CFPB explains that a payoff amount includes interest through the day you pay off the loan and may include unpaid fees or a prepayment penalty. With Scottsdale single-family homes selling for a median $1,225,000 and 96.8% of list price in October (Phoenix REALTORS), knowing your real payoff tells you what you'll actually walk away with.

How Selling With a Mortgage Works, Step by Step

  1. Estimate your payoff before you list; call your servicer or check your online account
  2. List and accept an offer; the purchase price must cover your payoff and costs, or you'll need a plan for the gap
  3. Escrow opens and the title company orders a payoff statement
  4. Title search finds every recorded lien: first mortgage, HELOC, second loan, judgments
  5. Closing: escrow wires each payoff from your proceeds
  6. Release: your lender records a release or reconveyance of the deed of trust

The Payoff Statement: Your Real Number

  • Written request: under Regulation Z, your servicer must send an accurate payoff statement within a reasonable time, and no more than seven business days after a written request, with limited exceptions
  • Good-through date: payoffs are calculated as of a specific date; if closing slips, escrow updates it
  • Daily interest: the CFPB notes payoff includes interest due through the payoff date
  • Fees and penalties: unpaid fees and any prepayment penalty are included; check your note

Net Proceeds, Simplified

Sale price - payoff(s) - seller closing costs - agreed concessions = your proceeds

Escrow handles the math on your settlement statement. For typical seller costs, see closing costs for sellers in Arizona and what it costs to sell a house in Arizona.

Taxes: IRS Publication 523 lets you exclude up to $250,000 of gain ($500,000 married filing jointly) if you owned and lived in the home at least two of the last five years. More in our Arizona capital gains guide.

Special Situations

A HELOC or second mortgage

Every lien must be paid or released. With a HELOC, ask the lender to freeze and close the line as part of the payoff, so it can't be drawn again before closing.

Owing more than the house is worth

You can bring cash to closing or seek a short sale, where the lender agrees in writing to accept less than full payoff. Arizona's A.R.S. 33-707 specifically covers releases for less than full satisfaction when agreed in writing.

A low rate you'd hate to give up

Freddie Mac had the 30-year fixed at 7.11% on Nov. 12, 2026, versus 6.24% a year earlier. Some FHA and VA loans can be assumed by a buyer; see assumable mortgages in Arizona.

After Closing: Make Sure the Lien Is Released

Under A.R.S. 33-712, a lender that receives full payment and fails to record a release within 30 days can be liable for actual damages. If it still hasn't recorded one more than 30 days after your certified-mail request, it can owe an additional $1,000. Keep your settlement statement and confirm the release on the county record.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you sell a house if you still have a mortgage?

Yes. Escrow pays off your loan from the proceeds at closing.

How does selling a house with a mortgage work?

Your servicer issues a payoff statement, escrow wires it at closing, and the lender records a release.

Is my payoff amount the same as my loan balance?

No. It includes interest through the payoff date and any unpaid fees or prepayment penalty.

What if I owe more than my house is worth?

Bring cash to closing or ask your lender about a short sale. Get tax advice first.
