How Much Down Payment Do You Need for a House in Arizona? Minimums by Loan Type, the 20% Question and Real Phoenix and Scottsdale Math

How much down payment do you need for a house in Arizona? As little as 3% on a conventional loan, 3.5% on an FHA loan, or nothing on a VA loan. Twenty percent is a target for avoiding mortgage insurance, not a requirement. On Phoenix's October 2026 median single-family price of $481,998, that's roughly $14,500 at 3% or $16,900 at 3.5%.

Scottsdale is a different conversation. With a citywide single-family median of $1,225,000, a 20% down payment still leaves a $980,000 loan, above Maricopa County's 2026 conforming limit of $832,750, which pushes many buyers into jumbo financing.

Data note: Loan minimums are from Fannie Mae (97% LTV options), HUD (FHA Myths vs. Facts) and VA.gov. PMI rules are from the CFPB. The county loan limit is from FHFA's 2026 list. Prices are from Phoenix REALTORS (October 2026). Rates are from Freddie Mac's PMMS (Nov. 12, 2026). Read November 19, 2026.

Minimum Down Payment by Loan Type

  • Conventional (Fannie Mae 97% LTV): 3% down. HomeReady has income limits of 80% of area median income; 97% LTV Standard has no income limit but requires at least one first-time homebuyer
  • FHA: 3.5% in most cases, per HUD, and that money may come from gifts, grants or down payment assistance
  • VA: no down payment as long as the sales price isn't higher than the appraised value, and no PMI, though a funding fee may apply
  • Jumbo (above $832,750 in Maricopa County): set by each lender, often higher

What Those Percentages Look Like in Dollars

Phoenix: $481,998 median single-family price (October 2026)

Monthly principal and interest at 7.11%, the Freddie Mac 30-year average for Nov. 12, 2026:

  • 3% down: about $14,460 down; about $3,145/month
  • 3.5% down: about $16,870 down; about $3,129/month
  • 5% down: about $24,100 down; about $3,080/month
  • 10% down: about $48,200 down; about $2,918/month
  • 20% down: about $96,400 down; about $2,594/month

These are principal and interest only, before taxes, insurance and mortgage insurance.

Scottsdale: $1,225,000 median single-family price

  • 20% down: $245,000, leaving a $980,000 loan
  • To stay at or under the $832,750 conforming limit: about $392,250 down, or roughly 32%

Quick math for common price points

  • $400,000 home: $12,000 at 3% · $14,000 at 3.5% · $20,000 at 5% · $80,000 at 20%
  • $500,000 home: $15,000 at 3% · $17,500 at 3.5% · $25,000 at 5% · $100,000 at 20%
  • $750,000 home: $22,500 at 3% · $26,250 at 3.5% · $37,500 at 5% · $150,000 at 20%

Where the money can come from

HUD notes that FHA's 3.5% minimum may come from your own funds, gifts, second mortgages or grants through down payment assistance programs. Conventional programs have their own gift rules, so ask your lender before moving money between accounts.

Should You Put 20% Down?

The case for 20%

  • No PMI on a conventional loan; the VA notes PMI is usually required below 20%
  • Lower monthly payment and more equity from day one

The case for less

  • Getting in sooner instead of saving for years
  • Keeping cash reserves for repairs, furnishings and emergencies
  • PMI isn't forever. The CFPB explains you can request cancellation at 80% of the original value and that it generally ends automatically at 78% if you're current. More in how to remove PMI

My usual advice: decide on the monthly payment you're comfortable with, keep a healthy reserve, then put the rest down.

Don't Forget the Cash Beyond the Down Payment

  • Earnest money when your offer is accepted (see how much earnest money in Arizona)
  • Closing costs and prepaid taxes and insurance
  • Reserves some loan programs require

Assistance programs can help. Start with first-time home buyer programs in Arizona or buying with no down payment.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much down payment do you need for a house in Arizona?

As little as 3% conventional, 3.5% FHA or 0% VA.

What is the minimum down payment for a conventional loan?

3% through Fannie Mae's 97% LTV options, with eligibility rules.

How much down payment do you need to avoid PMI?

Generally 20% on a conventional loan.

How much down payment for a $500,000 house?

About $15,000 at 3%, $17,500 at 3.5% or $100,000 at 20%.
