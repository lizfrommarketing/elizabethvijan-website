Assumable Mortgages in Arizona: How Taking Over a Seller's FHA or VA Loan Works

What is an assumable mortgage? It's a home loan a buyer can take over from the seller, keeping the existing interest rate, balance and remaining term. In practice, the loans that are assumable in Arizona are mostly FHA and VA loans, and the buyer still has to be approved by the loan servicer. Most conventional loans have a due-on-sale clause that requires payoff when the home sells.

Why buyers ask: when a seller's loan carries a lower rate than today's market, assuming it can lower the monthly payment. The trade-off is cash: the buyer usually needs to cover the difference between the purchase price and the remaining loan balance, in cash or with a second loan.

Assumable Mortgages at a Glance

  • FHA loans: assumable with a creditworthiness review of the buyer (24 CFR 203.512)
  • VA loans: assumable when the loan is current, the buyer is creditworthy and the buyer assumes full liability
  • Conventional loans: usually include a due-on-sale clause, which federal law allows lenders to enforce, with limited exceptions
  • What transfers: the rate, balance and remaining term
  • What doesn't: the seller's equity. The buyer pays that difference at closing

How a VA Loan Assumption Works

VA Circular 26-23-10 lays out the rules:

Approval

  • The loan must be current
  • The buyer must qualify on credit and take on full liability for the loan
  • Servicers with automatic authority must decide within 45 days of a complete package, and a denied buyer can appeal to VA within 30 days

Fees

  • VA funding fee: 0.5% of the loan balance, unless the buyer is exempt (for example, veterans receiving VA compensation for a service-connected disability)
  • The funding fee is paid at closing and can't be financed into the assumed loan
  • Processing fee: capped at $300 for servicers with automatic authority, or $250 if VA approves the assumption

The Entitlement Question Sellers Must Understand

  • Without substitution: if the buyer isn't a veteran using their own entitlement, the seller's VA entitlement stays tied to that loan until it's paid off. That can limit the seller's next VA purchase
  • With substitution: an eligible veteran buyer who will occupy the home and has enough entitlement can substitute theirs, which restores the seller's entitlement

How an FHA Loan Assumption Works

  • Free assumability: federal rules bar FHA lenders from restricting assumptions except as the rules allow (24 CFR 203.512)
  • Credit review: at least one person acquiring ownership must be found creditworthy under HUD standards
  • Occupancy: lenders can't approve a transfer to a buyer who won't use the home as a primary residence (or a secondary residence HUD permits), so investors generally can't assume
  • Processing fee: HUD's FHA INFO 2024-30 raised the maximum fee a lender may charge for processing an assumption from $900 to $1,800

Why Most Conventional Loans Aren't Assumable

The Garn-St Germain Act (12 U.S.C. 1701j-3) lets lenders enforce due-on-sale clauses. It also lists exemptions for homes with fewer than five units, including transfers:

  • To a relative after the borrower's death
  • To a spouse or children who become owners
  • Under a divorce decree or property settlement
  • Into a living trust where the borrower remains a beneficiary

These exemptions protect families. They don't make a conventional loan assumable for an unrelated buyer.

Writing an Assumption Into an Arizona Contract

  • The Arizona REALTORS resale contract includes "Assumption" as a financing type and a Loan Assumption addendum
  • Build in time: the VA's 45-day decision window can run longer than a typical close
  • Plan the gap: confirm how you'll cover the equity difference before you write the offer
  • Ask for the loan details early: the rate, balance, payment and loan type from the seller's statement

If an assumption doesn't pencil, compare a rate buydown or price reduction. First-time buyers should also review Arizona down payment programs.

Pros and Cons

For Buyers

  • Pro: a potentially lower rate than a new loan
  • Pro: VA funding fee of 0.5% on an assumption
  • Con: more cash needed for the seller's equity
  • Con: a longer, servicer-driven timeline

For Sellers

  • Pro: a feature that can make the listing stand out
  • Con: with a VA loan, entitlement may stay tied up without substitution
  • Con: you depend on the servicer's approval schedule

FAQ: Assumable Mortgages in Arizona

What is an assumable mortgage?

A loan the buyer takes over, keeping the rate and remaining term.

Can you assume a VA loan if you are not a veteran?

Yes, with approval. The seller's entitlement stays tied up.

How much does it cost to assume a mortgage?

VA: 0.5% funding fee plus up to $300. FHA: up to $1,800.

How long does a loan assumption take?

VA servicers have 45 days after a complete package.
