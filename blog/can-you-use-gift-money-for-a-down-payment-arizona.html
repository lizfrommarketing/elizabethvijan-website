Can You Use Gift Money for a Down Payment? Gift Letters, Who Can Give and the 2026 Tax Rules for Arizona Buyers

Can you use gift money for a down payment? Yes. On a conventional loan, Fannie Mae lets gift funds from a qualifying donor cover all or part of your down payment, closing costs and reserves on a home you'll live in or a second home. On a one-unit primary residence, there's no minimum you have to contribute from your own money.

What it takes: a signed gift letter, proof the money moved from the donor to you or to escrow, and a donor who fits the rules. For the giver, the IRS annual gift exclusion is $19,000 per recipient in 2026, and most families never owe gift tax at all.

Data note: Loan rules are from Fannie Mae's Selling Guide, B3-4.3-04 Personal Gifts (published Nov. 4, 2026). Tax figures are from the IRS gift tax FAQs and 2026 Form 709 instructions. Rates are from Freddie Mac's PMMS, Nov. 25, 2026. Read November 27, 2026. FHA, VA and other programs have their own gift rules; ask your lender.

Who Can Give You Down Payment Money?

Fannie Mae calls them acceptable donors:

Relatives

  • Your spouse, child or other dependent
  • Anyone related to you by blood, marriage, adoption or legal guardianship

Non-relatives with a family-like relationship

  • A domestic partner, or a relative of your domestic partner
  • Your fiancé or fiancée
  • A former relative
  • Someone with a long-standing familial-like or mentorship relationship with you

Not allowed: a donor who is, or is affiliated with, the builder, the developer, the real estate agent or any other interested party to the transaction. A seller who is also a relative can give a gift of equity, which Fannie Mae allows when the seller isn't affiliated with another interested party.

Can a Gift Cover the Whole Down Payment?

It depends on the property and your loan-to-value ratio:

  • 80% LTV or less (20% or more down), one- to four-unit primary home or second home: no minimum contribution from you. The gift can cover everything
  • Over 80% LTV, one-unit primary residence: still no minimum contribution. The gift can cover everything
  • Over 80% LTV, two- to four-unit primary home or second home: you must put in 5% from your own funds first; gifts can supplement after that
  • Investment property: gifts are not allowed

For how much to put down in the first place, see how much down payment you need in Arizona.

The Gift Letter: What It Must Say

Fannie Mae requires a letter signed by the donor that includes:

  • The actual or maximum dollar amount of the gift
  • A statement that no repayment is expected
  • The donor's name, address, telephone number and relationship to you

If the money comes from a trust or estate set up by an acceptable donor, the donor signs and lists the trust or estate account.

Proving the Money Moved

The lender must verify the funds, either still in the donor's account or already transferred to yours. Fannie Mae lists acceptable proof such as:

  • The donor's check and your deposit slip
  • The donor's withdrawal slip and your deposit slip
  • Evidence of an electronic transfer to your account or to the closing agent
  • The donor's check to the closing agent, or a settlement statement showing it was received

If the money isn't transferred before closing, the donor must send it to the closing agent by electronic transfer, certified check, cashier's check or other official check.

Tip: don't deposit a big gift as cash. A wire with a clear paper trail is much easier to document.

Living together? If the donor has lived with you for the past 12 months and both of you will use the new home as your principal residence, Fannie Mae treats the gift as your own funds for its minimum contribution rule. Expect to document the shared address.

The Tax Side for the Giver

  • 2026 annual exclusion: $19,000 per recipient. Each spouse gets one, so a married couple can give $38,000 to one person, and twice that to a married couple, without a taxable gift
  • Over the exclusion? The donor generally files IRS Form 709 by April 15 of the next year
  • Owing tax is rare: amounts above the annual exclusion count against the donor's lifetime basic exclusion, $15 million in 2026
  • 2027: the IRS lists the annual exclusion at $20,000

The recipient doesn't pay income tax on a gift. Talk to a tax professional for your situation.

Why It Matters at Today's Rates

Freddie Mac's 30-year fixed averaged 7.21% on Nov. 25, 2026. A larger down payment means a smaller loan and a lower monthly payment, and on a conventional loan, putting 20% down generally means no private mortgage insurance. See how to remove PMI if you're already paying it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you use gift money for a down payment on a house?

Yes, on a primary residence or second home under Fannie Mae's rules.

What does a gift letter for a mortgage need to say?

The amount, that no repayment is expected, and the donor's contact details and relationship.

Do my parents have to pay tax on a down payment gift?

Usually not. The 2026 annual exclusion is $19,000 per recipient.

Can gift money be used for closing costs?

Yes, along with the down payment and reserves.
