Arizona Property Tax vs. Other States: How Arizona Compares With California, Texas, Florida, Illinois and Nevada

How does Arizona property tax compare with other states? Arizona is among the lowest in the country: the Tax Foundation puts its effective property tax rate on owner-occupied homes at 0.48%, compared with 0.70% in California, 0.78% in Florida, 1.40% in Texas and 1.88% in Illinois. Maricopa County is lower still, at 0.44%.

Why it stays low: Arizona caps the growth of a home's taxable value at 5% a year, and a sale doesn't reset it. That's a key difference from California, where Proposition 13 resets value at purchase.

Data note: Effective rates are from the Tax Foundation's 2026 state tax pages and its Property Taxes by State and County, 2026, based on 2024 U.S. Census data. Arizona rules are from the Arizona Constitution, Arizona Revised Statutes and the Maricopa County Assessor. California rules are from the California State Board of Equalization. Read November 26, 2026. General information, not tax advice.

Effective Property Tax Rates by State

Property taxes paid as a share of owner-occupied home value, per the Tax Foundation (2024 data):

  • Illinois: 1.88%
  • Texas: 1.40%
  • Florida: 0.78%
  • California: 0.70%
  • Nevada: 0.50%
  • Colorado: 0.50%
  • Arizona: 0.48%

Maricopa County: median home value $452,800, median property taxes paid $1,983, effective rate 0.44% (2024, five-year Census estimate).

What That Looks Like on a $700,000 Home

An illustration only, applying each statewide average effective rate:

  • Arizona (0.48%): about $3,360 a year
  • Nevada (0.50%): about $3,500
  • California (0.70%): about $4,900
  • Florida (0.78%): about $5,460
  • Texas (1.40%): about $9,800
  • Illinois (1.88%): about $13,160

Real bills depend on the home's assessed value, local districts, bonds and overrides, and exemptions. For a new California purchase, the bill is usually tied to the price you pay, so it can run well above the statewide average.

How Arizona Property Tax Works

  • Two values: the assessor sets a full cash value and a limited property value (LPV). Since tax year 2015, property taxes are based on LPV
  • 5% cap: since tax year 2015, the Arizona Constitution limits LPV to the lesser of full cash value or 5% above the prior year's LPV
  • 1% ceiling: the Constitution caps primary taxes on residential property at 1% of its limited value. Bonds, special districts and voter-approved overrides are outside that cap
  • Assessment ratio: owner-occupied homes are assessed at 10% of value under A.R.S. § 42-15003, before tax rates apply
  • Sales don't reset it: the Maricopa County Assessor says LPV is recalculated only for specific events, such as construction of 15% or more of value, splits or a change in use. A change in occupant isn't one

What that means for buyers: when you buy in Arizona, you generally step into the seller's LPV. Your first bill isn't based on your purchase price.

How California Compares

From the Board of Equalization's overview of Proposition 13:

  • Rate: 1% plus voter-approved debt
  • Value resets at purchase: property is reassessed to market value on a change in ownership or new construction
  • 2% cap: after that, annual increases in the base-year value are generally limited to 2%
  • Homeowners' exemption: $7,000 off taxable value for an owner-occupied primary home

Long-time California owners often pay very little. Recent buyers pay on today's prices. That's why a move from California can feel very different depending on when you bought there. More in moving to Arizona from California.

Don't Forget Income Tax

Property tax is only part of the bill. Per the Tax Foundation:

  • Arizona: flat 2.5% income tax
  • California: 1% to 13.3%
  • Illinois: flat 4.95%
  • Texas, Florida and Nevada: no individual income tax

Texas and Florida trade higher property taxes for no income tax. Arizona is low on both. See does Arizona have a state income tax?

Frequently Asked Questions

Is property tax higher in Arizona or California?

California's is higher on average: 0.70% vs. 0.48%.

How does Arizona property tax compare with Texas?

Texas's effective rate, 1.40%, is about three times Arizona's.

Does Arizona reassess property when it sells?

No. A sale alone doesn't reset limited property value.

What is the property tax rate in Arizona?

It varies by district. The statewide effective rate is 0.48%.
