Quitclaim Deed in Arizona: How It Works, What It Costs and When to Use One

What is a quitclaim deed in Arizona? It's a deed that hands over whatever interest you have in a property, with no promise that the title is good. Arizona's statutory form is short: "I hereby quit claim to A.B. all my interest." A warranty deed adds, "and I warrant the title against all persons whomsoever."

When is it used? Mostly for transfers where no money changes hands: between spouses, into or out of a living trust, among family, or to clear up a title issue. It is not the deed used in a normal sale. The Arizona REALTORS resale contract calls for the seller to convey by warranty deed.

Data note: Deed forms and rules come from Arizona Revised Statutes (A.R.S. 33-401, 33-402, 33-411, 11-480, 11-1133, 11-1134, 25-214, 12-1103). Fees come from the Maricopa County Recorder. Mortgage rules reference 12 U.S.C. 1701j-3. No market statistics are included in this post.

Quitclaim Deeds in Arizona at a Glance

  • What it transfers: whatever interest the signer holds, if any
  • Title warranty: none
  • Must be: in writing, signed by the grantor and notarized (A.R.S. 33-401)
  • Recording fee in Maricopa County: $30
  • Affidavit of Property Value: required unless an exemption applies and is noted on the deed
  • Mortgage: a quitclaim does not remove anyone from the loan

Quitclaim vs. Warranty Deed

Quitclaim Deed

  • Transfers all my interest, whatever that turns out to be
  • Gives the new owner no recourse against the signer if title problems appear
  • Best when parties already know and trust the title history

Warranty Deed

  • Promises the title against all persons
  • Standard in Arizona resale purchases
  • Paired with an owner's title insurance policy. See who pays title insurance in Arizona

Special Warranty Deed

Common with lenders, builders and estates, a special warranty deed generally covers only the time the seller owned the property. Ask your title officer which deed your transaction calls for.

When Arizonans Use a Quitclaim

  • Adding or removing a spouse on title
  • Moving a home into a living trust
  • Divorce settlements, where one spouse keeps the house
  • Clearing a cloud on title: A.R.S. 12-1103 lets an owner ask someone with an adverse claim to sign a quitclaim, with a $5 tender, before filing a quiet title action

A Note for Married Owners

Arizona is a community property state. A.R.S. 25-214 generally requires both spouses to join in conveying community real estate. When a married buyer takes title as sole and separate property, the resale contract notes the title company may require a disclaimer deed from the spouse. See is Arizona a community property state?

How to File a Quitclaim Deed in Maricopa County

1. Prepare the Deed

A.R.S. 11-480 sets recording standards:

  • A caption naming the document, such as "Quitclaim Deed"
  • An original with original signatures
  • No larger than 8.5 x 14 inches, 10-point type or larger
  • A 2-inch top margin on the first page and half-inch margins elsewhere
  • The legal description of the property

2. Sign Before a Notary

A.R.S. 33-401 requires the deed to be signed by the grantor and acknowledged before an officer authorized to take acknowledgments.

3. Add the Affidavit or an Exemption

Under A.R.S. 11-1133, the Recorder will refuse a deed without an Affidavit of Property Value unless it shows an exemption under 11-1134. Exemptions include:

  • A quitclaim for no monetary consideration
  • Nominal-consideration transfers between spouses, parent and child, grandparent and grandchild, or siblings
  • Transfers between spouses to create community property with right of survivorship
  • Transfers to a trustee with only nominal consideration

The exemption must be noted on the face of the deed. Recorder staff can't choose one for you.

4. Record It

Pay the $30 recording fee. Recording matters: A.R.S. 33-411 says an unrecorded deed isn't notice to later buyers who pay value without knowing about it.

The Mortgage Doesn't Move With the Deed

  • A deed changes ownership; the loan stays with the borrowers who signed it
  • To be released, the loan generally needs a refinance, payoff or lender-approved assumption
  • Garn-St Germain (12 U.S.C. 1701j-3) limits due-on-sale enforcement on homes with fewer than five units for transfers such as to a spouse or children who become owners, under a divorce decree, or into a living trust where the borrower stays a beneficiary

FAQ: Quitclaim Deeds in Arizona

What is a quitclaim deed in Arizona?

A deed transferring your interest, with no title warranty.

How much does it cost to file a quitclaim deed in Maricopa County?

$30 to record, plus any preparation fees.

Does a quitclaim deed remove you from the mortgage?

No. The loan is a separate contract.

Is a quitclaim deed the same as a warranty deed?

No. Only a warranty deed promises good title.
