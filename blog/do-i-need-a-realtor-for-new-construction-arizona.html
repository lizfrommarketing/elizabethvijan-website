Do I Need a Realtor for New Construction in Arizona? What Buyers Should Know Before Visiting a Builder

Do you need a Realtor to buy new construction in Arizona? No law requires one, but the builder's sales consultant represents the builder, not you. Your own agent compares the builder's price with resale homes, reads the builder's contract, tracks deadlines and helps hold the builder to its promises before and after closing.

The timing tip that matters most: many builders have agent-registration rules, so bring your agent, or ask about the policy, on your very first visit to a model home. Waiting until you're ready to sign can mean going without representation.

Who Works for Whom

  • Builder's sales consultant: represents the builder's interests and pricing
  • Builder's preferred lender and title company: often tied to incentives, but you can compare outside quotes
  • Your agent: works under a buyer broker agreement with duties to you; see what a buyer broker agreement is

Ask early how your agent will be paid. It's negotiable and should be spelled out in writing.

Arizona Rules Every New-Build Buyer Should Know

The public report

Under A.R.S. 32-2183, a subdivider can't sell lots in a subdivision without first obtaining a public report from the Arizona Department of Real Estate. The subdivider must give each initial buyer a copy before the buyer signs any offer to purchase, taking a receipt, and failure to deliver the report to the buyer can make the sale rescindable. Read it closely; among other items, the statute requires a disclosure when lots sit near a military airport or under a military training route.

Two years to file with the ROC

  • A.R.S. 32-1162: a complaint with the Registrar of Contractors for a new home build must be filed within two years after the earlier of close of escrow or actual occupancy
  • A.R.S. 12-1365: your contract must disclose that right in at least 10-point bold type, and you aren't considered to have received the notice unless you initial it

Right to repair before a lawsuit

Under A.R.S. 12-1363, before filing a construction-defect action, a buyer must send the seller written notice by certified mail. The seller may inspect, and must send a good-faith written response within 60 days, which can include an offer to repair, replace or pay.

The outer deadline

A.R.S. 12-552 generally bars contract-based construction claims more than eight years after substantial completion, with a limited extension for injury or latent defects found in the eighth year, up to nine years. It doesn't shorten an express written warranty.

Inspect a Brand-New Home Anyway

New doesn't mean flawless. Common inspection points:

  • Pre-drywall: framing, plumbing and wiring before they're covered
  • Final walkthrough: grading, roof, HVAC, appliances and finishes
  • Before warranty ends: a second look so issues get reported in time

For typical fees, see home inspection cost in Arizona.

New Build or Resale? Run the Numbers

Builder incentives often focus on rates and closing costs, so compare them against what resale sellers are offering.

  • Rates: Freddie Mac's 30-year average was 7.11% on Nov. 12, 2026
  • Scottsdale resale supply: 1,480 single-family homes for sale in October 2026, a 4.2-month supply, per Phoenix REALTORS
  • Negotiating room: Scottsdale resale homes closed at 96.8% of list price on median, after 75 days on market

With that much resale inventory, a seller-paid buydown on an existing home can rival a builder's incentive. See rate buydown vs. price reduction.

Questions to Ask the Builder

  1. What's included in the base price, and what are lot premiums?
  2. What's the warranty in writing, and how do I submit claims?
  3. What are the incentives with and without the preferred lender?
  4. When is the estimated completion, and what happens if it slips?
  5. What are the HOA dues and rules?
  6. Can my inspector attend pre-drywall and final walkthroughs?

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a realtor for new construction in Arizona?

Not legally, but the builder's rep works for the builder. Bring your agent on the first visit.

Should I get a home inspection on new construction?

Yes, ideally pre-drywall, at final walkthrough and before the warranty ends.

How long do I have to file a complaint against a builder in Arizona?

Two years after close of escrow or occupancy, whichever comes first, under A.R.S. 32-1162.

What is a public report in Arizona?

A required subdivision disclosure. Without it, a sale can be rescindable under A.R.S. 32-2183.
