How to Find a Water Leak in Your House: The Water Meter Test, Toilet Dye Check and Other Steps for Scottsdale and Phoenix Homes

How do you find a water leak in your house? Start at the water meter. Turn off everything that uses water, write down the reading and the sweep hand's position, wait at least 30 minutes without using any water, and read it again. If anything moved, something is pulling water through the meter. That's Scottsdale Water's own meter flow test, and it works for any metered home in the Valley.

Then narrow it down: toilets, faucets and irrigation are the usual suspects. EPA WaterSense says a typical household's leaks can waste more than 9,300 gallons a year, and fixing easy leaks can save about 10% on water bills.

Data note: The meter test, shut-off valve location and high-bill causes are from Scottsdale Water's Maintenance, Prevention and Troubleshooting page. Leak volumes and the dye test are from EPA WaterSense. Read November 25, 2026. General information, not plumbing advice.

Signs You Might Have a Leak

  • A higher-than-normal water bill. Scottsdale Water says the most common causes are a malfunctioning irrigation system or a leaky pipe or toilet
  • High winter use. EPA suggests checking a colder month, such as January or February; a family of four using more than 12,000 gallons a month could have serious leaks
  • A toilet that runs or refills on its own
  • Damp spots, warm areas on a tile floor, or the sound of running water when everything is off
  • Soggy patches or unusually green spots in the yard

Step 1: The Water Meter Test

From Scottsdale Water:

  1. Make sure nothing is using water: faucets, dishwasher, washer, irrigation, pool fill, water softener
  2. Find the meter, usually at the front of the property near the street or sidewalk. Lift the lid with a screwdriver, and watch for critters
  3. Record the odometer-style numbers and the position of the sweep hand
  4. Use no water for at least 30 minutes, then read it again
  5. Any change, or a moving sweep hand or leak triangle, means something on the property is pulling water

EPA suggests an even longer version: read the meter before and after a two-hour window with no water use.

Step 2: Inside or Outside?

Next, find the owner's shut-off valve. Scottsdale Water says it's adjacent to the water meter box. A common way to split the problem:

  • Close the house shut-off and watch the meter again
  • Meter still moving? The leak is likely between the meter and the house, or in irrigation and other lines that aren't downstream of that valve
  • Meter stops? The leak is likely inside the house

Scottsdale Water and the Arizona Municipal Water Users Association publish a step-by-step Smart Home Water Guide for this kind of detective work.

Step 3: Check the Usual Suspects

Toilets

EPA's dye test: put a few drops of food coloring in the tank. If color appears in the bowl within 10 minutes, you have a leak. Flush afterward to avoid staining. EPA says flappers should be checked periodically and replaced at least every five years. Here's how to fix a running toilet.

Faucets and showerheads

  • A faucet dripping once per second wastes more than 3,000 gallons a year, per EPA
  • A showerhead leaking 10 drips per minute wastes more than 500 gallons a year
  • Check fittings and gaskets for water on the outside of pipes

See how to fix a leaky faucet.

Irrigation

EPA says a leak 1/32 of an inch in diameter, about the thickness of a dime, can waste about 6,300 gallons a month. Walk your drip lines and sprinkler heads while the system runs.

Water softeners and appliances

Scottsdale Water notes softeners and other water-using appliances can affect pressure. Bypass one and recheck if you suspect it.

When to Call a Pro

Call a licensed plumber or leak-detection specialist when:

  • The meter moves but you can't find the source
  • You suspect a leak under the slab or behind a wall
  • The leak is on the service line between the meter and house

Frequently Asked Questions

How can I tell if I have a water leak in my house?

Do the meter test: no water use for 30 minutes, then compare readings.

How do I check my toilet for a leak?

Food coloring in the tank; color in the bowl within 10 minutes means a leak.

How much water does a leak waste?

More than 9,300 gallons a year for an average household, per EPA.

Where is my water shut-off valve in Scottsdale?

Next to the water meter box, per Scottsdale Water.
