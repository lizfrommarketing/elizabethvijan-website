Home Equity Agreement vs. HELOC: How Home Equity Investments Work and What They Really Cost

What's the difference between a home equity agreement and a HELOC? A HELOC is a loan: you borrow against your equity and pay interest monthly. A home equity agreement (also called a home equity investment, or HEI) gives you cash now in exchange for a single repayment later tied to your home's value. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau found that repayment amount often grows 19.5% to 22% per year in the early years.

The short answer: a home equity agreement can work for an owner who can't qualify for or carry a monthly payment. For most owners who can, a HELOC or cash-out refinance tends to cost less. Here's how to compare them honestly.

Data note: All figures are from the CFPB's Issue Spotlight "Home Equity Contracts: Market Overview" (Jan. 15, 2025). Read November 11, 2026. Contract terms vary by company; your agreement controls.

How a Home Equity Agreement Works

  • You receive: an upfront lump sum from a company
  • You give: a share of your home's future value, secured by a lien
  • Monthly payments: typically none
  • Repayment: one lump sum at the end of the term, usually 10 to 30 years, or at a trigger like a sale
  • You still pay: property taxes, insurance, maintenance and all selling costs
  • Who uses them: the CFPB found the median customer was in their 50s, and roughly 90% or more had a mortgage ahead of the contract

The names vary. CFPB notes these are marketed as home equity investments, home equity agreements or shared equity agreements.

What It Really Costs: The CFPB Examples

Three years in

On a $50,000 upfront payment, one company's calculator showed a repayment of $68,045 (if the home lost an average 1% a year) to $71,538 (if it appreciated by any amount) after three years.

Ten years: contract vs. HELOC

CFPB compared the same $50,000 as a home equity contract and as a HELOC at 9%, interest-only for 10 years:

Home equity contract:

  • Monthly payment, years 1-10: $0
  • Owed at year 10: $94,074 to $215,892, depending on home prices
  • Total paid over 10 years: $94,074 to $215,892

HELOC:

  • Monthly payment, years 1-10: $375 in interest
  • Owed at year 10: the original $50,000
  • Total paid over 10 years: $95,000

The CFPB's takeaway: the contract was more expensive if the home appreciated, and cheaper only if the home's value fell at least 5% over 10 years. That comparison also excludes origination fees, which CFPB says are typically much higher for home equity contracts; two companies it cited charged 3% and 4.9% at closing.

Pros and Cons

Potential advantages

  • No monthly payment
  • Available to some homeowners with low credit scores or no income, per CFPB's review of marketing
  • Cash for debt consolidation or home improvements, the two most common uses CFPB found

Risks the CFPB flagged

  • Unpredictable repayment that can run into the hundreds of thousands
  • Non-standardized disclosures, making comparison hard
  • Refinancing friction with the contract's lien in place
  • Appraisal disputes, at the start or at settlement
  • Forced sale risk: owners who can't pay at the end may need to sell or face foreclosure

In CFPB's review of 38 consumer complaints, 6 of 21 published narratives called the products "predatory."

How a HELOC Compares

  • Structure: a revolving line of credit secured by your home
  • Payments: monthly; often interest-only during the draw period
  • Cost: interest on what you draw, plus closing costs
  • Approval: CFPB notes HELOCs often have stricter underwriting
  • Scale: about 1.2 million HELOCs were originated in the four quarters ending mid-2024, versus about 11,000 home equity contracts securitized by the four largest companies in the first 10 months of 2024

For more on borrowing against equity, see HELOC vs. cash-out refinance.

Questions to Ask Before You Sign

  1. What's the maximum I could owe, and is there a cap?
  2. How is the starting home value set, and is it discounted?
  3. What fees are due at closing?
  4. What happens at the end of the term if I can't pay?
  5. Will this make it harder to refinance my mortgage?
  6. Have I compared a HELOC, a cash-out refinance, a reverse mortgage or selling and renting?
“Any lien on your home comes due at the closing table, and a home equity agreement is no exception. Frequently Asked Questions

What is a home equity agreement and how does it work?

Cash now in exchange for a share of your home's value later, repaid in one lump sum at the end of the term or when you sell.

Is a home equity agreement better than a HELOC?

Rarely on cost. In CFPB's example, it was cheaper only if the home lost at least 5% of its value.

What are the pros and cons of a home equity agreement?

No monthly payment and looser approval, versus high, unpredictable costs and possible forced sale.

Does a home equity agreement affect selling my house?

Yes. It's typically settled from your proceeds at closing.
