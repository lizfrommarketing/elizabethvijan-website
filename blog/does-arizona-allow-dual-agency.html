Does Arizona Allow Dual Agency? Limited Representation, Written Consent and What It Means for Buyers and Sellers

Does Arizona allow dual agency? Yes. Arizona lets one broker, or two licensees in the same brokerage, represent both the buyer and the seller when every party gives prior written consent. Arizona REALTORS® materials call this dual agency or limited representation.

Is Arizona a dual agency state? In practical terms, yes—with a consent requirement. Acting for more than one party without knowledge or written consent can trigger discipline under Arizona law. With consent, it is lawful, but the agent's advocacy is limited.

Dual Agency in Arizona at a Glance

  • Allowed with prior written consent of all parties
  • Arizona REALTORS® label: dual agency / limited representation
  • Can be one licensee or two licensees in the same firm
  • READE form (Real Estate Agency Disclosure and Election) helps define who represents whom
  • Consent to Limited Representation form documents the dual role
  • Dual agents must still deal honestly and disclose certain material facts
  • Dual agents generally do not advocate for one side against the other
  • Compensation is not set by law and is fully negotiable

What the Law Actually Says

Arizona does not ban representing both sides. It bans doing it in the dark.

Statute: consent or face discipline

Under A.R.S. § 32-2153(A)(2), the Real Estate Commissioner may discipline a licensee who “acted for more than one party in a transaction without the knowledge or written consent of all parties to the transaction.” Read the other way: representing more than one party is contemplated when parties have knowledge or give written consent.

Commissioner’s rule: written consent first

A.A.C. R4-28-1101(F) (Duties to Client) is direct: a salesperson or broker “shall not accept compensation from or represent more than one party to a transaction without the prior written consent of all parties.”

The same rule, R4-28-1101(A)–(B), says a licensee owes a fiduciary duty to the client, must deal fairly with other parties, and must disclose in writing information that materially or adversely affects the consideration to be paid—including inability to perform, material defects, and liens or encumbrances.

Those duties still apply when the firm is in a limited-representation role.

How Arizona REALTORS® Frame It

Arizona REALTORS® counsel describes dual agency (limited representation) as happening when:

  • One broker individually represents both buyer and seller, or
  • Two salespeople in the same brokerage firm represent the two sides

That article states dual agency is lawful with prior written consent, and points parties to the Consent to Limited Representation form.

Per that guidance, a dual agent should exercise reasonable skill and care, deal honestly, and disclose inability to perform, material defects, and possible liens (aligned with R4-28-1101(B)). A dual agent generally must not advocate for one side against the other or disclose confidential leverage—buyer will pay more, seller will take less, alternate financing, repair concessions, or motivating factors—without informed consent.

The Forms You’ll See

READE: Real Estate Agency Disclosure and Election

The READE form is a disclosure and election of agency—not an employment agreement. Arizona REALTORS® note that using it helps prevent undisclosed dual agency. It explains exclusive representation duties and introduces limited representation when both sides are in the same firm.

Consent to Limited Representation (November 2024 sample)

The sample Consent form states that Broker, through the named licensee(s), will represent both parties, and that neither Broker nor the licensee(s) can represent one party’s interests to the exclusion or detriment of the other.

The form warns of conflicts in loyalty, obedience, disclosure and confidentiality; blocks sharing the other party’s price flexibility without written authorization; still requires disclosure of facts that materially and adversely affect consideration; and states “Compensation to the Broker is not set by law, is fully negotiable…” If no contract is signed or the deal fails to close, the Consent terminates.

What This Means If You’re Buying or Selling

Buyers and sellers both trade full advocacy for convenience when the same firm is on both sides. Ask who represents you before you share your top number or bottom line. Either side can say no—consent is yours to give or withhold. Request the Consent form early and read it calmly.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Arizona a dual agency state?

Yes. Arizona allows dual agency / limited representation with prior written consent of all parties.

What is limited representation in Arizona real estate?

It is dual agency under Arizona REALTORS® forms: the same brokerage represents buyer and seller, with limited advocacy and confidentiality rules spelled out in the Consent form.

Do I have to agree to dual agency in Arizona?

No. Without your written consent, a licensee should not represent both sides. You can choose exclusive representation instead.

What must a dual agent still disclose in Arizona?

Material facts that affect consideration—such as defects, liens, and a party’s possible inability to perform—under R4-28-1101, plus honest dealing. Confidential price flexibility still needs written authorization to share.
