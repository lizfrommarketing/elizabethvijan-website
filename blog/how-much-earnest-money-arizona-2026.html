How Much Earnest Money Is Typical in Arizona? What Buyers Put Down, Risk and Get Back

How much earnest money is typical in Arizona? There's no set amount in the standard contract. The Arizona REALTORS resale contract simply leaves a blank for the dollar figure, and buyer and seller negotiate it. The right number depends on the price, how competitive the home is, and how strong you want your offer to look.

Is it refundable? Often, yes, if you cancel on time under a contingency. That includes the inspection period (10 days by default), the appraisal contingency and the loan contingency. If you breach and don't cure, the seller can keep it. Know your deadlines before you wire a dollar.

What Earnest Money Is (and Isn't)

  • What it is: a good-faith deposit showing the seller you're serious
  • Where it goes: the AAR contract has it deposited with the escrow company or a broker's trust account, not the seller
  • How you pay: the form offers personal check, wire transfer or other
  • What happens at closing: it's credited to you, typically toward your down payment and closing costs
  • What it isn't: your down payment itself, and not a fee you lose just for changing your mind on time

How to Choose Your Amount

Since there's no standard percentage, think it through with your agent:

  • Competition: a home with several offers may call for a stronger deposit
  • Price point: a deposit that looks serious on a condo may look light on a Paradise Valley estate
  • Your timeline: a short inspection period plus a larger deposit signals confidence
  • Documentation: Fannie Mae says large deposits, or ones that exceed what's customary for the area, should be closely evaluated by the lender. Keep the money seasoned in your account.

A number your lender can easily verify is better than a bigger one that slows underwriting.

When You Get It Back

Under the Arizona REALTORS resale contract:

Inspection Period

  • 10 days by default
  • If you disapprove items and cancel on time, the earnest money goes back to you

Appraisal Contingency

  • If the appraisal comes in below the price, you have five days after notice of the value to cancel and get it back. See what to do when an appraisal comes in low.

Loan Contingency

  • Deliver notice that you couldn't get loan approval no later than three days before close of escrow to get it back
  • Not locking a rate, or not having your down payment funds, is not an unfulfilled loan contingency

The Big Rule

  • "An unfulfilled contingency is not a breach of Contract."

When You Could Lose It

  • Missed deadlines that turn into a breach
  • Failure to close after a cure notice. The AAR contract gives three days to cure; after that the earnest money is subject to forfeiture
  • Liquidated damages: if the buyer breaches, the seller may keep the earnest money as their sole right to damages
  • Waived contingencies: if you waive the appraisal contingency, a low appraisal alone won't get your money back

If There's a Dispute

  • The AAR contract lets the escrow company release the money under the contract terms, in its sole discretion
  • A Phoenix REALTORS sample contract (Dec. 2024) says escrow won't release disputed funds without a signed distribution agreement or a court judgment, and sends disputes to mediation first
  • Escrow may also file an interpleader action and let a court decide

Protect Your Wire

The Arizona contract warns that criminals hack email accounts of brokers and title companies to send fake wire instructions. Before you send earnest money:

  • Call escrow at a number you found yourself, not one in an email
  • Confirm the account details verbally
  • Never act on "updated" instructions without a verified call

FAQ: Earnest Money in Arizona

How much earnest money is typical in Arizona?

No fixed amount; it's negotiated and depends on price and competition.

Is earnest money refundable in Arizona?

Yes, if you cancel on time under a contingency. A breach can mean losing it.

Does earnest money count toward my down payment?

Yes. It's credited at closing; your lender will verify where it came from.

Who holds the earnest money in Arizona?

The escrow company or a broker's trust account.
