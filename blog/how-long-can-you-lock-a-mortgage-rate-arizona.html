How Long Can You Lock a Mortgage Rate? Lock Periods, Extension Costs and When to Lock in Arizona

How long can you lock a mortgage rate? Most lenders offer rate locks of 30, 45 or 60 days, and sometimes longer, according to the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau. Your locked rate holds as long as you close within that window and nothing material changes in your application.

Why it matters this month: Freddie Mac's 30-year fixed average jumped to 7.28% on November 19, 2026, from 7.11% a week earlier. On a $400,000 loan, that one-week move adds about $46 a month in principal and interest, or about $552 a year.

Data note: Rate lock guidance is from the CFPB's "What's a lock-in or a rate lock on a mortgage?" page. Rates are from the Freddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey for November 19, 2026. Payment math is principal and interest only on a 30-year fixed loan. Read November 23, 2026.

How a Rate Lock Works

  • Mortgage rates can change daily, sometimes hourly
  • A lock means your rate won't change between the offer and closing, as long as you close on time and your application doesn't change
  • Some lenders lock as part of the Loan Estimate; others don't. Check the top of page 1 of your Loan Estimate to see if your rate is locked and until when
  • Without a lock, your rate can change at any time

Typical Lock Periods

30 days

Common for a standard purchase with a clean file and a set closing date. Make sure the lock runs past your contract's closing date, not just up to it.

45 to 60 days

Useful if you expect a longer escrow, a later move-in date or extra underwriting time.

Longer locks

Some lenders offer longer periods, often for new construction. Expect them to cost more.

The CFPB notes your Loan Estimate won't show what an extension would cost or what you're paying for the lock period. Ask.

What Can Still Change a Locked Rate

The CFPB lists common reasons:

  • Changing the loan type or down payment
  • An appraisal that comes in higher or lower than expected
  • A credit score change, such as opening a new card or missing a payment
  • Income the lender can't document, like overtime or bonus

Rule of thumb: from application to closing, don't open new credit, make large unexplained deposits or change jobs without calling your lender first.

The Cost of Waiting: This Week's Numbers

  • 30-year fixed: 7.28% on Nov. 19, 2026, up from 7.11%; 6.26% a year ago
  • 15-year fixed: 6.66%, up from 6.45%

Principal and interest on a 30-year fixed loan:

  • $400,000: about $2,691 at 7.11% vs. $2,737 at 7.28%
  • $500,000: about $3,364 vs. $3,421, or about $690 more per year

Freddie Mac releases the survey on Thursdays at noon Eastern. The next release falls on Thanksgiving week, so watch your lender's daily pricing too.

Six Questions to Ask Your Lender

Straight from the CFPB's list:

  1. What does it mean if I lock my rate today?
  2. What rate lock time frame does this Loan Estimate provide?
  3. Is a shorter or longer lock available, and at what cost?
  4. What if my closing is delayed and the lock expires?
  5. Are there conditions under which my locked rate could still change?
  6. If I lock and rates go down, what happens? (Ask whether a float-down option exists)

When Arizona Buyers Usually Lock

  • After you're under contract with a known closing date
  • When the payment fits your budget at today's rate
  • With a cushion: choose a period that covers the closing date plus a few days

Closing around the holidays? Title, lender and county offices keep shorter hours, so build in time. See should I close in December or January and how long it takes to close in Arizona.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long can you lock a mortgage rate?

Typically 30, 45 or 60 days, sometimes longer, per the CFPB.

Can my rate change after I lock?

Yes, if your loan type, down payment, appraisal, credit or income changes.

What happens if my rate lock expires before closing?

You may pay to extend or move to the current rate. Ask your lender up front.

Should I lock my mortgage rate now or wait?

No one can predict rates. If you're under contract and the payment works, locking removes the risk.
