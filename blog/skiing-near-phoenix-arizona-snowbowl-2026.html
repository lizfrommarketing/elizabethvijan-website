Skiing Near Phoenix: Arizona Snowbowl Is Open for 2026-27. Lift Tickets, Terrain, Shuttles and Tips From Scottsdale

Is there skiing near Phoenix? Yes, and it's open now. Arizona Snowbowl, 14 miles north of Flagstaff, opened for the 2026-27 season on Thursday, Nov. 19, a day ahead of schedule, and is running daily. Opening terrain was advanced runs off the Arizona Gondola, with more to come as snowmaking and storms allow.

Planning a holiday ski day from Scottsdale? Buy online before Cyber Monday, Nov. 30, at midnight for Snowbowl's lowest lift ticket prices of the season. Kids 12 and younger ski free with a Power Kids Pass.

Data note: Season, terrain and ticket details come from Arizona Snowbowl's Nov. 19, 2026 opening announcement and its mountain information and directions pages. Directions are from the U.S. Forest Service, Coconino National Forest. Ticket starting prices are from KVOA and Arizona's Family (Nov. 17-19, 2026). Read November 27, 2026. Conditions change daily; check the snow report before you drive.

Snowbowl at a Glance

  • Where: 9300 N. Snowbowl Road, Flagstaff, on the western slope of Mount Humphreys, Arizona's highest point
  • Opened: Thursday, Nov. 19, 2026, at 9 a.m.
  • Status: open daily for skiing and riding
  • Preseason snow: 21 inches before opening, per Snowbowl
  • Average season: mid-November through early to late May
  • Average snowfall: 260 inches

Mountain Stats

  • Base elevation: 9,200 feet
  • Lift-served elevation: 11,500 feet
  • Hike-to elevation: 12,000 feet
  • Total vertical rise: 2,800 feet
  • Skiable acres: 777
  • Runs: 61; the longest is 2 miles
  • Lifts: 8, including a high-speed six-person chairlift, a high-speed detachable combination lift, four fixed-grip chairlifts and two surface conveyors

For perspective, Camelback Mountain's summit is 2,704 feet, per the City of Phoenix. Snowbowl's base sits about 6,500 feet higher.

What's Open Early Season

At opening, Snowbowl listed advanced terrain off the Arizona Gondola:

  • Spur Catwalk
  • Frontier
  • Lower Bowl
  • Logjam
  • Wild Turkey
  • Round Up

Beginners and intermediates: watch the snow report. Snowbowl says its snowmaking and grooming teams are working to open terrain for all ability levels as conditions allow.

Lift Tickets, Passes and Deals

  • Starting prices: 2026-27 lift tickets start at $9 on select dates when bought in advance, per KVOA and Arizona's Family
  • Buy-early window: lowest prices of the season online and in advance through Cyber Monday, Nov. 30, at midnight, plus $10 Snowbowl Cash on dates marked orange in the ticket calendar
  • Kids: 12 and younger ski free all season with a Power Kids Pass, no parent purchase required
  • New this winter: tickets and passes load to a Power Access Card; print yours at a kiosk at Agassiz, Hart Prairie or Fort Valley Lodge, and keep it for future visits

Getting There From Scottsdale

  • Drive: take Interstate 17 north to Flagstaff. From Flagstaff, the Forest Service says to drive 7 miles north on U.S. Highway 180 to the Snowbowl Fort Valley Center, then 7 more miles to the lodge and lifts
  • Shuttles from Phoenix: Snowbowl lists Alpine Express and Desert Snow Connection as preferred services
  • Shuttle from Flagstaff: Snowbowl runs a free shuttle from Flagstaff from late December to February
  • Road conditions: Snowbowl posts them with its snow report; check before you leave the Valley

Tips for a Desert-Dweller Ski Day

  • Pack for real cold. Base elevation is 9,200 feet. Layers, gloves and goggles matter even after a warm morning in Scottsdale
  • Start early. Leave time for the drive, parking, printing your Power Access Card and picking up rentals
  • Hydrate and take it slow the first hour at altitude
  • Know your level. Early-season terrain is advanced only; save beginner days for later in the season
