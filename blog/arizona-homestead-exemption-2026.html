Does Arizona Have a Homestead Exemption? 2026 Guide for Scottsdale and Phoenix Homeowners

Does Arizona have a homestead exemption? Yes. Under A.R.S. 33-1101, the equity in the home you live in is protected from attachment, execution and forced sale by most creditors, up to a statutory base of $400,000. That figure adjusts for inflation every January 1; applying the statute's formula, legal.com calculates $437,600 for 2026.

Is it a property tax break? No, and this is the most common mix-up. Arizona's homestead exemption is creditor protection. The tax benefit for living in your home comes from your primary residence (class 3) classification, which unlocks the state homeowner rebate of up to $600 per parcel.

Data note: Statutory details come from the Arizona Revised Statutes on azleg.gov. The 2026 adjusted amount is a calculation published by legal.com using BLS CPI-U data, not an official state table. This is general information, not legal or tax advice.

Arizona Homestead Exemption: Key Facts

  • Amount: $400,000 statutory base, adjusted annually starting Jan. 1, 2024
  • Adjustment method: percentage change in the national CPI-U from August to August, rounded up to the nearest $100
  • Calculated amounts (legal.com): $414,700 (2024), $425,200 (2025), $437,600 (2026)
  • Who qualifies: a person 18 or older residing in the state
  • What it covers: your interest in a house, condominium, cooperative or mobile home (plus land) that you live in
  • How you get it: automatically, by operation of law
  • Couples: one homestead exemption per married couple
  • After a sale: protection follows the identifiable cash proceeds for 18 months, or until you buy a new home, whichever comes first. It does not extend to refinance proceeds
  • Bankruptcy: the amount in effect on the filing date applies

What the Homestead Exemption Does Not Protect Against

Under A.R.S. 33-1103, the exemption doesn't block a forced sale tied to:

  • Your mortgage or deed of trust (consensual liens)
  • Mechanics' liens for labor or materials
  • Child support or spousal maintenance arrearages that meet the statute's conditions
  • Recorded judgments, when your equity exceeds the exemption amount

When You Own More Than One Home

  • The exemption attaches to the home you live in
  • If several properties could qualify, a creditor can demand that you designate one within 30 days; otherwise you may need to record a homestead claim (A.R.S. 33-1102)

Property Tax Breaks for Arizona Homeowners (the Real "Homestead" Savings)

Primary Residence (Class 3) and the Homeowner Rebate

  • Owner-occupied primary residences receive additional state aid that reduces school district taxes
  • The reduction equals 50% of the qualifying school tax rate, capped at $600 per parcel (A.R.S. 15-972)
  • The Assessor reviews class 3 status. If you don't respond to a review, the parcel can be reclassified as class 4 (rental), and misclassification carries a civil penalty equal to the prior year's state aid (A.R.S. 42-12052)
  • Arizona's constitution also caps primary property taxes on residential property (Article IX, Section 18); if a bill would exceed that cap, the county applies a credit (A.R.S. 15-972)

Exemptions for Widows, Widowers, Disability and Veterans

  • $4,188 assessed-value exemption for qualifying widows, widowers and people with a total and permanent disability, subject to income limits and a total assessment cap of $28,459 (A.R.S. 42-11111)
  • Full exemption for the primary residence of a veteran with a 100% service-connected disability rating
  • $4,188, scaled by disability percentage, for other qualifying disabled veterans

What This Means When You Buy or Sell

  • Buying: make sure the county records your home as your primary residence so you receive the class 3 rebate
  • Selling: the homestead protection follows your sale proceeds for 18 months, which matters if you're between homes
  • Moving into a rental: a home you no longer live in generally stops qualifying for class 3 treatment
  • Estate and creditor questions: an Arizona attorney can apply the current exemption amount to your situation

FAQ: Arizona Homestead Exemption

Does Arizona have a homestead exemption?

Yes. A.R.S. 33-1101 protects home equity up to a $400,000 base, automatically.

How much is the homestead exemption in Arizona in 2026?

About $437,600, per legal.com's calculation using the statute's CPI-U formula. Confirm with an attorney.

Does the Arizona homestead exemption lower property taxes?

No. The tax benefit is the class 3 homeowner rebate, up to $600 per parcel.

Does Arizona have a homestead exemption for seniors?

Not a separate creditor exemption, but widow/widower, disability and veteran property tax exemptions exist under A.R.S. 42-11111.
