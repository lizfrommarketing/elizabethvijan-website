ARM vs. Fixed-Rate Mortgage: Which Makes Sense for Arizona Buyers With Rates Back Above 7%?

ARM or fixed: which is better right now? A fixed-rate mortgage is the safer choice if you plan to stay put, because the rate never changes. An adjustable-rate mortgage (ARM) starts lower, about a point lower in this week's MBA survey, and can make sense if you expect to sell or refinance before the fixed period ends. The catch: after that period, your rate follows the market.

This week's numbers: Freddie Mac's 30-year fixed average jumped to 7.28% on November 19, 2026, from 7.11% a week earlier. The Mortgage Bankers Association's average 5/1 ARM rate was 6.38%, versus 7.35% for its 30-year fixed conforming average.

Data note: Fixed-rate averages are from Freddie Mac's Primary Mortgage Market Survey (PMMS) for November 19, 2026. ARM and conforming rates, points and ARM share are from MBA's Weekly Applications Survey released November 18, 2026 (week ending November 13). Definitions are from the CFPB. Phoenix and Scottsdale prices are from Phoenix REALTORS, October 2026. Read November 20, 2026.

How Each Loan Works

Fixed-rate mortgage

  • Interest rate set at closing and never changes, the CFPB explains
  • Principal and interest stay the same for the life of the loan
  • Common terms: 30-year (7.28% on Nov. 19) and 15-year (6.66%), per Freddie Mac

Adjustable-rate mortgage

  • Starts with an initial fixed period; a 5/1 ARM is fixed for five years, then adjusts
  • New rate = index + margin, subject to rate caps, per the CFPB
  • The margin is set in your loan agreement, won't change after closing and can be negotiated
  • Caps can differ for the first adjustment, later adjustments and the life of the loan

One more data point: Freddie Mac's PMMS stopped publishing ARM rates in November 2022, which is why ARM pricing here comes from MBA.

Rates This Week, Side by Side

  • 30-year fixed (Freddie Mac PMMS, Nov. 19): 7.28%
  • 15-year fixed (Freddie Mac PMMS, Nov. 19): 6.66%
  • 30-year fixed conforming (MBA, week ending Nov. 13): 7.35%, 0.79 points
  • 5/1 ARM (MBA): 6.38%, 1.07 points
  • ARM share of applications (MBA): 10.8%

Note the points: the ARM's lower rate came with slightly higher average points, so compare full Loan Estimates, not headline rates.

What It Means on a $400,000 Loan

Illustration using MBA's average rates, principal and interest only:

  • 30-year fixed at 7.35%: about $2,756 a month
  • 5/1 ARM at 6.38%: about $2,497 a month
  • Difference: about $259 a month, or roughly $15,500 over five years
  • Balance after five years: about $373,900 on the ARM versus $377,900 on the fixed loan

The reset risk

Suppose your ARM's caps allowed 2 points at the first adjustment and 5 points over the life of the loan (an example; your caps will be in your loan documents). At year six:

  • At 8.38%: about $2,981 a month on the remaining balance
  • At the 11.38% lifetime ceiling: about $3,768 a month

If that worst case would break your budget, the ARM's early savings aren't worth it.

Who Each Loan Fits

A fixed rate usually fits if you:

  • Plan to stay more than five to seven years
  • Want a payment that never changes
  • Are stretching to qualify and can't absorb a higher payment later

An ARM may fit if you:

  • Expect to move or sell within the fixed period, such as a job relocation
  • Can comfortably afford the payment at the maximum capped rate
  • Are buying a higher-priced home: Scottsdale's single-family median was $1,225,000 in October; Phoenix's was $481,998

Loans above MBA's conforming threshold of $832,750 are jumbo loans; see our Arizona jumbo loan limit guide.

Questions to Ask Your Lender About Any ARM

  • What index is the loan tied to, and what is the margin?
  • What are the first, periodic and lifetime caps?
  • How often does the rate adjust after the initial period?
  • Is there a rate floor, and can the payment ever go down?
  • What are the points and fees compared with a fixed-rate quote?
  • What would my payment be at the highest possible rate?

The CFPB suggests finding out how high or low your rate and payment can go with each adjustment, and how frequently it will adjust, before you choose. Get those answers in writing on your Loan Estimate.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between an ARM and a fixed-rate mortgage?

A fixed rate never changes; an ARM's rate adjusts after an initial period, based on an index plus a margin, within caps.

Are ARM rates lower than fixed rates right now?

Yes. MBA's 5/1 ARM average was 6.38% versus 7.35% for a 30-year fixed conforming loan.

Is an ARM a good idea in 2026?

It can be for shorter time horizons, if you can afford the capped worst case.

How does an ARM adjust?

Index plus margin, limited by first, periodic and lifetime caps.
