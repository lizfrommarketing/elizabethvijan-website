Is Paradise Valley a Good Place to Live? One-Acre Lots, No Town Property Tax and What Homes Cost in 2026

Is Paradise Valley a good place to live? It's a small, almost entirely residential town between Phoenix and Scottsdale where most lots must be at least one acre, the Town levies no property tax of its own, and Camelback and Mummy mountains frame the views. The trade-offs are price, with an October 2026 single-family median of $5,300,000, and a do-it-yourself approach to services like trash collection.

Quick facts: incorporated in 1961, 15.4 square miles, about 12,774 residents (2025 estimate), and home to 9 resorts and 3 golf courses, according to the Town.

Data note: Town facts are from the Town of Paradise Valley's Basic Town Facts and Town History pages and its Resident's Guide (PDF). Market figures are from the Phoenix REALTORS® Local Market Update for Paradise Valley, October 2026, the latest published. Read November 26, 2026.

Why Paradise Valley Exists

In the late 1950s, as Phoenix and Scottsdale looked to expand, residents worried about being annexed into subdivisions and shopping centers. The Town's history says their goals were to keep one house per acre, keep the area residential, and keep government minimal. Maricopa County granted incorporation on May 24, 1961, with about 2,000 residents.

That founding idea still shapes daily life.

The Town by the Numbers

From the Town's Basic Town Facts:

  • Population: 12,658 (2020 Census); 12,774 (2025 estimate)
  • Households: 5,046 (2020 Census)
  • Land area: 15.4 square miles
  • Elevation: 1,165 feet
  • Also in town: 9 resorts, 14 places of worship, 11 public and private schools, 3 golf courses and 4 medical centers

Zoning: Why Lots Are So Large

The Resident's Guide says the Town is primarily zoned for single-family homes, one home per lot:

  • R-43: most of the Town; lots of at least one acre
  • R-175: four-acre lots in some areas
  • R-35, R-18 and R-10: smaller lots in limited areas
  • Non-conforming lots: some R-43 lots under an acre that predate annexation remain legal

Other uses, including resorts, religious facilities and private schools, need a Special Use Permit after public hearings.

Taxes and Fees

  • No town property tax: the Resident's Guide says the Town does not assess a primary or secondary tax. You still pay county, state, school and special district taxes
  • How the Town is funded: sales and bed taxes from resorts, a share of state sales, income and gas taxes, permit and franchise fees, and investment interest
  • Fire service fee: since January 2013, billed monthly and based on a home's square footage
  • Other utility-style fees: sewer service and alarm monitoring are run as enterprise funds

Everyday Logistics

  • Trash: the Town doesn't provide collection. Residents subscribe to one of five licensed private haulers, with pickup days set by area
  • Water: City of Phoenix, EPCOR or Berneil Water Company, depending on the address
  • Sewer: City of Phoenix or City of Scottsdale service, or a septic system
  • Schools: most of the Town is in Scottsdale Unified, with Kiva and Cherokee elementary schools inside town limits. Areas west of Tatum Boulevard and south of Lincoln Drive are served by Creighton Elementary and Phoenix Union High School districts
  • Cell service: delivered through a distributed antenna system hidden in 42 faux cactus
  • Trails: Camelback Mountain's Echo Canyon and Cholla trailheads are just over the line in Phoenix

What Homes Cost: October 2026

Phoenix REALTORS® data for Paradise Valley:

Single-family

  • Median sales price: $5,300,000, up 20.5% from $4,400,000
  • Year-to-date median: $4,400,000, up 6.0%
  • Closed sales: 17
  • Days on market: 85
  • Percent of list price received: 95.4%
  • Inventory: 190 homes, 7.1 months of supply

Townhouse and condo

  • Year-to-date median: $650,000
  • Inventory: 55 units, 9.3 months of supply

With 17 sales, one month's median swings easily; the year-to-date figure is steadier. Supply above 7 months and 95.4% of list price point to a market where buyers can negotiate.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Paradise Valley its own city?

Yes. It's an incorporated town, established May 24, 1961.

Is Paradise Valley part of Scottsdale or Phoenix?

Neither. It's a separate town bordered by both.

Does Paradise Valley have property tax?

The Town levies none, but county, state and school taxes still apply.

Why is Paradise Valley so expensive?

Mostly one-acre zoning and limited supply. The October 2026 median was $5.3 million.
